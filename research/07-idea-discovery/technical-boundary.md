## Official rules and timetable

[UGREEN's Devpost rules](https://ugreenhackathon.devpost.com/rules), viewed 2026-10-26, say the Round 1 deadline is **November 15, 2026, 11:45 p.m. Pacific Time** (November 16, 1:45 a.m. Mexico City time). Submit a PDF answering ten questions in at most 500 English words total, with exceptions stated in the rules, and a ZIP of at most 10 MB containing an initial `SKILL.md` and optional directly linked Markdown references. Round 1 does not require NAS operation, a video, or a separate code repository.

Up to 30 finalists receive **one assigned day** between November 22 and November 28, from 12:00 a.m. to 11:59 p.m. Pacific, for exclusive remote access to an iDX6011 Pro (64 GB). The final Skill package and video are due **November 30, 11:45 p.m. Pacific Time** (December 1, 1:45 a.m. Mexico City). The same project must run on the assigned NAS; the video must show actual operation, be public on YouTube, and use English narration or complete English subtitles. See the [official rules](https://ugreenhackathon.devpost.com/rules) and [resources FAQ](https://ugreenhackathon.devpost.com/resources). Check the official rules again before submitting.

Use the official tool, supported NAS capabilities, registered experts, and Uliya standard. The package cannot contain executable scripts, separate manifests, binaries, or other unsupported files. The rules do not categorically ban third-party models: they are allowed when authorized, subject to rights, terms and disclosure requirements; the local model is encouraged, not mandatory. The Skill cannot assume arbitrary direct access, general-purpose runtimes, a custom backend, or an undocumented way to call a third-party service. New core workflows must have been developed during the hackathon; prior ideas may be used. AI tools and use must be disclosed per the FAQ. The proposal must disclose third-party components, licenses, external services, and data flows where applicable.

## Parent and child responsibilities

Primary local references: `SKILL.md`, `references/cli-usage-rules.md`, `references/expert-orchestration.md`, `references/safety-and-approval.md`, and `references/completion-and-errors.md` inside the installed kit. The parent calls `delegate --target <agent-id> --instruction '<task>'`; each instruction supplies authorized scope and constraints. The child selects and executes its registered CLI/action workflow. The parent does not invoke `cli.invoke`, copy private parameters, bypass approval, or pretend a local static check ran on the NAS. The catalog does document an `ok/data` or `ok/error` CLI invocation envelope, but it is not a complete guarantee of the expert-to-parent evidence and provenance fields.

A downstream task needs a completed upstream business result. Explicitly handle `completed`, `failed`, `cancelled`, `waiting_user`, and `waiting_approval`; preserve partial results, stop dependencies on missing data, and never mark pending as completed. For writes, approval and completion are performed by the child workflow; catalog approval values are not proof that a real approval succeeded.

Document operations have finite processing parameters: catalog `ug-document:qa` and `reconcile` max-files allow 1–200 and default to 60; `extract` allows 1–500 and defaults to 60. These are action parameters selected by the child, not an instruction for the parent to call its CLI. Discovery output can be limited, inaccessible, or partial. The kit does not guarantee pagination, complete file enumeration, source-level evidence, reliable truncation flags, or an exhaustive inventory. Specify bounded authorized retrieval and label coverage unknown when no actual records establish otherwise. Do not infer that a file does not exist just because it was not returned.

## Feasibility by direction

| Direction | Plausible documented composition | Crucial unverified dependency |
|---|---|---|
| Find a potentially important discrepancy or evidence gap across invoices, correspondence and records | `file-agent` inventory → `document-agent` extraction/reconciliation → `knowledge-agent` evidence → parent response | Exact cross-expert source-path/quote preservation, coverage signals, and factual precision. A missing file is not proof that it never existed. |
| Personal oral-history companion with selected photos and voice memos | `file-agent` discovery, `photo-agent` vision/OCR, `knowledge-agent` audio transcription; parent drafts a sourced story | Audio/visual formats, transcription, accessible source references, permissions, and real multimodal handoff. Treat assembled prose as a draft, not recorded fact. |
| Creator source-material and rights checklist | Photo search/vision, file metadata, document extraction and evidence collection | Metadata alone cannot establish license or identity; linking clips to contracts and absent rights records may fail. No rendered video or slideshow action is documented by this catalog. |
| Reversible file organization proposal | File inventory, hashes, document extraction; optional approved `file-workflow:batch-move` | Whether approval preview and move outcomes show reliable exact details; catalog `restore` does not prove point-in-time restoration. Safer first demonstration is a proposal only. |
| Cross-NAS continuous replication, automation, persistent knowledge graph or timed alerts | No verified complete supported path identified in this kit | No catalog guarantee of multi-NAS replication, event subscriptions, scheduling, persistent graph database, external push, custom service, or interactive graph UI. Do not promise. |

A NAS may offer access to a larger, persistent, user-controlled archive and native authorized experts, but nothing here establishes that all inference and data flows are always local, nor that shared data is private from other NAS users. Validate the chosen model and integrations separately.

## Existing Skills and contest overlaps

The official [Skills Hub](https://skills.ugreen.com/skill-hub/) was linked by the contest but inaccessible to the research browser on 2026-10-26; a direct HTTP request reached a JavaScript-only app shell without listings, so its full listings could not be audited, so absence of a hit there is not evidence of novelty. The installed toolkit ships examples for duplicate reports, contract–invoice audit, finding PDFs, annual-photo organization, meeting notes, evidence extraction and surveillance-event queries. These are **toolkit examples**, not evidence that specific public Hub products are installed or available.

The [official project gallery](https://ugreenhackathon.devpost.com/project-gallery) stated that the managers had not published it as of the research date. Nevertheless, an individually accessible Devpost page, [ClaimReady Home Inventory](https://devpost.com/software/claimready-home-inventory), explicitly associates itself with this hackathon and describes joining photos, receipts and warranty records into an insurance inventory with source links, missing-proof lists, and file SHA-256 fingerprints. It is a **substantially overlapping** competitor to any insurance-claim or disaster inventory concept; it is a related-technology/different-outcome comparator for broader discovery. Its author’s statements are self-reported; this review does not independently verify working NAS execution, and the inaccessible gallery means public submissions cannot be comprehensively enumerated. A search miss is not proof of worldwide novelty.
