## Why family-recipe reconstruction

A family may have a recipe spread across a card, a conversation and notes. The concrete task is to reconstruct *one* recipe, keep distinct variants and produce a reviewable draft instead of inventing missing steps. This is a narrow use of a persistent collection that includes real multi-format handoffs and a user decision.

The problem is real enough to investigate: one [family recipe question on Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/FoodTech/comments/1r2w6ci/does_anyone_know_of_an_app_where_i_could_share_my/) asks for a way to digitize and share a grandmother's recipes. It is an anecdote, not a market-size estimate. Strong competitors already exist: [Touta](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/touta-family-recipe-keeper/id6755372509) describes photo, voice and audio recipe capture, and [Recipes We Share](https://recipesweshare.com/app) describes handwritten-card digitization and a shared family cookbook. These are publisher descriptions, not tests. The proposed NAS workflow's emphasis on conflicting variants and source trails is a design distinction to test, **not proof those products lack it** or that we invented family recipe reconstruction.

## UGREEN feasibility

The [installed catalog](../ugreen-capabilities/02-operation-catalog.json) documents file-agent discovery; document-agent reading/comparison; photo-agent OCR, visual inspection and album creation; knowledge-agent audio transcription and text saving. The package delegates only to those registered owners and uses explicit user consent and documented workflow approval for optional writes.

Real photo paths and identifiers, `add-item` encoding, album membership/order, OCR and transcription formats, returned provenance, save destinations, no-overwrite, approval resumption and UGREEN runtime are **unverified**. The first deliverable is the in-chat draft. Saved text or an album is optional and must stop if the child expert cannot establish the requested safe operation. There is no promise of offline inference, a custom GUI or a ready-to-cook recipe. The actual sample uses a synthetic card image and supplied transcript text, not a real audio transcription.
