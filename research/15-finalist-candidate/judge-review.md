# Simulated judge review — not official judging

Three independent fresh local Codex processes using the same `gpt-6.1-sol` low-effort configuration assessed the actual fictional outputs and selected original sources as owner, AI engineer and creativity judge. They were explicitly told no real device, approvals, expert returns or human feedback existed. Their equal-weight 25-point subdivisions are our interpretation of the four official categories, not an official rubric. Exact prompts, outcomes and logs are under `experiments/15-finalist-candidate/judge-reviews-v2/`. A = generic, B = frozen Skill.

| Simulated perspective | User value | Innovation | Technical quality | NAS fit/validation | Total | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Owner | 19 | 7 | 20 | 16 | 62/100 | REDESIGN |
| AI engineer | 20 | 8 | 19 | 13 | 60/100 | REDESIGN |
| Creativity | 21 | 8 | 20 | 12 | 61/100 | REDESIGN |

All three said G achieved essentially the same event, restricted, business and explicit prior-review outcomes. The creativity reviewer answered the key question directly: the judge sees a **contradiction/evidence table** and an earlier-to-current status table on request; no new interaction or actual NAS operation was demonstrated. They considered the material permission restriction useful, but cited ordinary file-aware AI, Nextcloud, ClaimReady, Publish-Safe Photo Review or Adobe DAM as meaningful competition. They flagged broken citations, possible overreading of PASS as authenticated approval and unknown UGREEN retrieval/permissions. Their scores are subjective model outputs, not a real jury, winning probability or independently established accuracy.

**Fairness and reviewer limitations:** an earlier `judge-reviews/` packet inadvertently omitted the actual delta comparison, so those first reviewers correctly said their supplied evidence lacked it. The second, reported packet included both real G/S delta outputs and the same actual earlier review; it is a post-hoc reassessment, not an independent blinded user test. Reviewers read a selected subset of files. They did not flag that the business report describes an invoice amount rather than explicitly labeling it revenue; both G/S nonetheless marked the revenue check PASS. An author audit identifies that semantic ambiguity. Some S responses refer to the Skill disclosure reference being absent from the **test archive**; the reference exists in the actual Skill package. Reviewers' comments on noisy/missing references concern this test presentation, not a demonstrated package failure. Reviewer report links themselves point into their ephemeral reviewer workspaces and need source mapping.

**Our decision differs from the simulated recommendations:** they proposed rerunning with a saved earlier review, but the post-hoc delta test they evaluated already did that for both G and S, with no distinctive Skill outcome. Simply fixing local links or adding a table does not resolve the missing product differentiation. Per the user's decision gates, recommend STOP for Readiness Delta as a standalone creative entry; keep the evidence and recommend the existing Project Rescue prototype only as a conditional fallback.
