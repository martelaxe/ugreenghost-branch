# Project Rescue: recover the right file, preserve its source

**User promise:** “Find the missing piece of my project using its surrounding context, and help me put one supported copy back without disturbing the original.” Keep the name Project Rescue: it works for both creative and business projects, while the subtitle explains the bounded behavior. It does not mean undeleting lost physical bytes.

The user authorizes a search scope and destination, then asks naturally. The workflow derives the absent dependency from an actual project record, compares versions and approval notes with candidate contents, and explains why the strongest candidate is suitable or why identity remains uncertain. If it can justify a single copy, it presents exact paths, source preservation, alternatives, uncertainty and impact. Only an exact owner approval, platform approval and established atomic no-overwrite semantics permit an action. Readback separates a completed copy from verified bytes and from application functionality.

The potentially memorable moment is recognizing that an oddly named source is both the missing approved item and already used by a different collection: copying leaves that use intact. An ordinary filename search may surface a misleading final-named draft. However, a competent generic AI can perform the same reasoning and Adobe relinking or backups may solve the user's situation faster. Neither novelty nor saved time is established. Limited search cannot prove all dependencies, and a match in descriptions is not proof of identity, authenticity, or successful application repair.
