# Focused prior-art check — 27 October 2026

## Three closest implementations

1. **[XTDB bitemporal database](https://docs.xtdb.com/about/time-in-xtdb.html)** maintains system time (when data entered its system) separately from valid time (when a fact is modeled as true) and supports queries such as `FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF`. It can reconstruct the database's recorded state and later corrections if the events were captured accurately; it does not by itself establish what an individual saw or believed. Our proposed two-view explanation is a presentation over similar temporal provenance, not a novel temporal storage mechanism. XTDB is an actual database; our exercise uses author-created Markdown snapshots.
2. **[ForeSci](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00644), Tian et al. (2026)** supplies cutoff-aligned offline knowledge bases, hides post-cutoff papers during generation and uses later material for evaluation. It is directly relevant to the physical separation of the THEN investigator. The benchmark addresses research forecasting rather than a consumer's personal decision archive; a reveal UI is not a new claim about information gating. Their paper also observes that relevant citations need not yield correct decisions.
3. **[VersionRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08109), Huwiler, Stockinger and Fürst (2025)** represents version sequences and content changes and routes retrieval according to version-sensitive questions. It addresses evolving documents and compares versions; its document-version state is not necessarily evidence of delivery to a specific decision-maker. A user-facing then/reveal/now story may combine version-aware retrieval with archive receipts, but that combination has not been shown unique.

Related mechanisms: [lakeFS](https://docs.lakefs.io/) supports data branches and reproducible commits; [GitHub's commit comparison](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/how-tos/commit-changes/comparing-commits) presents changes across commits; [TempRALM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13222) incorporates temporal relevance in retrieval. Snapshots and commits record a system state but are not, by themselves, proof that a specific person had access or read a record. [Roese and Vohs' hindsight-bias review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26168501/) notes that knowing the outcome may inflate perceived predictability; it cautions that visualizations can themselves heighten hindsight bias. This prototype does not establish any measured debiasing benefit.

**Novelty assessment:** availability-aware historical reconstruction, controlled holdout and time-sensitive retrieval already exist. The possible distinction is a consumer interaction that directly links a selected past interpretation to the exact later record that changes it, while exposing missing provenance. It may simply be a point-in-time query plus a comparative display. No broad originality, user-benefit, or psychological-effect claim is justified by this search.
