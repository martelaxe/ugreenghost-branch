## Repeated sources and groups

The previous research repeatedly revisited generic Codex comparisons, UGREEN catalog and static checker, Nextcloud Assistant, QNAP semantic search, existing photo or consent workflows, YoloFS and named skill benchmarks. These were reasonable controls but can bias attention toward textual reasoning. Groups: families, photographers, creators, archivists, small businesses and people inheriting accounts were considered; observations are mostly English-language technical online discussions. People working with oral recordings, household camera-event retention and cross-library media migration were less deeply studied as distinct groups.

Product categories already inspected include NAS search, document management, media curation, project relinking, backup/snapshot products and agent/skill systems. Media editing was considered but sound-based synchronizing and user-facing migration checks deserve closer comparison. No representative market survey, actual buyer study or real NAS testing supports a claim that the opportunity space is exhausted. Generic agents matching selected synthetic tasks supports only a narrow parity observation, not a conclusion that no Skill can add value.
