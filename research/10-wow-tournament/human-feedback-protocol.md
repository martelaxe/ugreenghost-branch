# Real-person concept-selection pilot — pending

No people have been contacted or asked to judge the concepts. Give 3–5 consenting participants the [anonymized fictional-excerpt packet](../../experiments/10-wow-tournament/human-packet/participant-packet.md), share the `human-packet/` folder including its neutral-label source files, while keeping [facilitator mapping](../../experiments/10-wow-tournament/facilitator-only-mapping.json) separate. The material uses actual local model output excerpts; labels do not announce concept names. They are a mix of different-length excerpts, and excerpts are not complete product demos, so preferences may reflect excerpt selection or format. The packet makes this limitation explicit.

Ask each person, “Which of these would make you most excited to own an AI NAS, and why?” They may choose **none**. The packet also asks which they would use, what felt surprising or generic, and whether they can solve the puzzle. Reveal the puzzle solution `BLUE 12` only after an answer. Do not imply the source material is their own or that the artifacts have run on a NAS. Keep the examples fictional and avoid collecting sensitive information.

Use different reading orders, for example ABCD, BCDA, CDAB, DABC, and ADCB; randomly assign orders and record them. For accessibility, offer reading aloud and enough time, and record any assistance. Ask which of their actual possessions or situations, if any, makes an idea attractive; do not push a particular winner. Record reasons, concerns, `none` selections, solve success, and whether they noticed citation/quote problems. A handful of convenience-sample responses can disconfirm a weak demo or suggest changes but cannot estimate market demand or statistically compare product quality. A fairer future test would show equal-duration, equally polished full demos from the same device and consented real archives, with order counterbalanced.
