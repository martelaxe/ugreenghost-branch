# Cross-domain interaction candidates

Research date: 2026-10-27. These are proposed interactions, not implemented UGREEN capabilities or global novelty claims. They use at least two reviewed mechanisms as analogies or actual design dependencies. E = event-anchored evidence (EM²Mem/OHMS), S = validated workflow state (SKILL.state), G = prerequisites (Graph-of-Skills), H = executable intervention (HASP), F = staged, mediated action (YoloFS), A = coarse-to-fine audio alignment (SyncSink), T = interval/retention evidence (Frigate), V = version and causality limits (lakeFS). Where only an analogy is possible, it is not a claim that the cited paper performs the user activity.

## 1. Field ecology: blind listening with capture-evidence gates

**Ask:** “Did the sounds at this spot change after we changed the garden? Help me listen without knowing which is which.” **Happen:** The system withholds condition labels, presents two recordings for an honest listener choice, then reveals source/capture conditions. If microphone or position is unknown, the comparison is blocked and the person gets a specific missing-input request. **Mechanisms:** E binds the audio to conditions; G checks prerequisite evidence; S represents `blocked → ready → listen → reveal`; H is an actual boundary in a real implementation, not a prompt. **More than search:** the user performs a blinded sensory comparison and is prevented from treating insufficient metadata as a result. **Change:** a defensible next observation, not a claim that a garden intervention caused anything. **UGREEN fragment:** `file-agent` can locate authorized records, `knowledge-agent` or `document-agent` can extract source descriptions; `media-agent` documents media search/control, but actual playable identifiers, concealment, sound sample handling and return fields are unknown. **Not supported:** validated blind player, randomization, programmatic gate, signal calibration, species detection, safe conclusions. Local proof exists, explicitly non-NAS. **Closest:** [blind ABX](https://github.com/jaakkopasanen/ABX), [Open Ecoacoustics](https://openecoacoustics.org/).

## 2. Accessible co-performance: compare the same stored sound by touch and hearing

**Ask:** “Can my friend who uses a tactile interface and I work out whether these two recordings share the same rhythm?” **Happen:** One person receives a haptic pattern, the other hears masked clips; each votes before the source is revealed. They play together without forcing one participant to rely on the other's description. **Mechanisms:** E ties each haptic/audio rendition to the same source interval; A could align repeated beats; S requires both votes before reveal; H would need to enforce separation. **Change:** an asymmetric cooperative sensory activity, not a transcript. **UGREEN fragment:** potentially retrieve and play an authorized media item if actual media-agent results support it. **Not supported:** audio feature extraction, device-specific haptic patterns, synchronized haptics, separated player sessions and accessibility validation. **Closest:** [Google audio-to-tactile](https://github.com/google/audio-to-tactile), [Sonic Inclusion](https://github.com/indjoov/sonic-inclusion). The specific two-person blind comparison was not established; accessibility usefulness requires disabled co-design, and vibration is not equivalent to hearing.

## 3. Physical science education: an uncertain measuring instrument

**Ask:** “Can we test whether this toy car rolls farther on surface A than surface B?” **Happen:** A stored user-authored protocol gives the class a randomized next surface, asks for a distance measurement, then refuses a conclusion when one run lacks the launch height; the pupils repeat that run. A visible physical experiment occurs, not an AI summary. **Mechanisms:** G prerequisites and S state; E connects observed measurement to a photo or entry; H requires a real gate for reliable enforcement. **UGREEN fragment:** retrieve protocol documents and request the next question in chat, possibly store an approved note. **Not supported:** actual randomization guarantee, sensor measurements, tamper-proof allocation, durable state, scientific validity or direct visualization. **Closest:** [StudyU](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9297132/), [N-of-1 design guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/n-of-1-study-comparative-studies). This applies experimental-design machinery to a toy experiment rather than health; a generic chatbot may match it.

## 4. Cooperative tabletop movement: audio cue exchange

**Ask:** “Turn our selected recordings into an activity where two people have to coordinate a sequence of taps without seeing each other's clues.” **Happen:** Each gets a different timing cue; the pair completes a physical rhythm without the system presenting the combined answer. **Mechanisms:** E for source and rights; S for turn state; A only if actual signal alignment is implemented. **Change:** joint physical performance, not media search. **UGREEN fragment:** possibly play selected known library audio after explicit user request; no evidence of separate synchronized streams. **Not supported:** private two-device sessions, latency management, beat detection or safe sound levels. **Closest:** established call-and-response and [AudioTools interactive co-improvisation](https://mashav.com/sha/praat/scripts/AudioTools_CallResponse.html). This looks too close to existing music games; reject pending much more distinctive behavior.

## 5. Speech accessibility: personalized sound-and-image communication board

**Ask:** “Use my approved pictures and recordings to make buttons for things I often need.” **Happen:** Touching a photo plays a personally selected phrase; the person communicates without needing to type. **Mechanisms:** E binds explicit approved sound/photo; S tracks board state; F describes a staged draft before approval. **Change:** a practical physical communication interface. **UGREEN fragment:** locate photos and audio; UI and reliable playback not documented. **Not supported:** accessible board UI, clinical suitability, device integration or automatic consent. **Closest:** [MyTalkTools](https://www.mytalktools.com/dnn/prod/home.html) supports recorded familiar voice and custom boards; [Sanoo](https://sanoo.app/) supports photo and speech buttons. **Reject:** central interaction substantially exists; disabled users must lead any accessibility design.

## 6. Physical spaces: transform sound through a captured room

**Ask:** “How would this instrument sound in the old auditorium?” **Happen:** The actual sound plays convolved with that room's measured acoustic response. **Mechanisms:** A for temporal alignment during measurement; E for a recording's location/time/source; signal convolution is additional outside the reviewed 12 mechanisms. **Change:** a playable physical-space model, not a description. **UGREEN fragment:** find recordings and metadata only. **Not supported:** verified impulse-response measurement, audio convolution or live audio. **Closest:** [university interactive auralization](https://mta.hmu.gr/en/research/in-brief-research/research-demos/auralisation/) lets users upload audio and choose a listening position; [Sonic Palimpsest](https://research.kent.ac.uk/sonic-palimpsest/impulse-responses/) supplies measured heritage-room responses. **Reject:** the central experience already exists.

## 7. Personal privacy and community: threshold release of a shared collection

**Ask:** “Keep this community recording locked until at least three of its five contributors actively join a listening session.” **Happen:** A protected recording becomes decryptable only when the threshold of independent key shares is supplied; a single NAS owner cannot unilaterally open it if designed correctly. **Mechanisms:** F stages release; S tracks participation; threshold cryptography is an additional primitive beyond the reviewed papers. **Change:** access depends on a group rather than a single file-owner switch. **UGREEN fragment:** can describe a proposed user consent workflow; no documented threshold encryption, trusted identity, cryptographic key handling or revocation. **Not supported:** actual security or enforced multi-person access. **Closest:** [TCFS threshold group sharing](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/events/usenix01/freenix01/full_papers/cattaneo/cattaneo_html/node10.html) and [Shamir implementation](https://github.com/privy-io/shamir-secret-sharing). **Reject as main idea:** substantially overlaps existing secret sharing and Experiment 17 consent-bearing objects; withdrawal cannot erase released copies.

## 8. Neighborhood citizen science: coordinated observation with blind independent reviewers

**Ask:** “Can two neighbors independently judge whether this unknown sample contains the same type of recurring sound before we look at the map?” **Happen:** Both receive the same anonymous sample without seeing each other's choice; reveal agreement and source conditions after both commit. The result is a human observation, not automatic animal identification. **Mechanisms:** E anchors common audio; S stores two independent commitments; H would prevent early reveal; T marks missing recording intervals. **Change:** shared observation becomes a reproducible social activity with an uncertainty record. **UGREEN fragment:** find the audio and collect a single chat response; potentially play a library item. **Not supported:** separate isolated clients, guaranteed blind independent votes, group state, haptic devices or network coordination. **Closest:** [Open Ecoacoustics](https://openecoacoustics.org/) and existing blinded listening systems. The exact household interaction is not verified as absent and may be an ordinary assembly of existing tools.

## 9. Everyday learning: a tabletop trial with masked conditions

**Ask:** “My child thinks the cardboard bridge is stronger folded. Help us test it without telling the person loading weights which design is which.” **Happen:** The pair prepares two coded samples, a third person records progressive weights, and only after both measurements does the code reveal. **Mechanisms:** S enforces commitments, G checks the same material/span/load steps, E ties photographs and measurements to coded samples. **Change:** makes an experiment people physically perform and exposes expectation bias. **UGREEN fragment:** retrieve selected notes/photos, hold a short conversational state and propose source-linked steps. **Not supported:** physically blinding people, inspecting safety or enforcing random assignment, reliable multi-party privacy, or an automatic causal conclusion. **Closest:** blinding and [N-of-1/crossover methodology](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/n-of-1-study-comparative-studies) are established; child activities need adult judgment and avoid unsafe loading. The real product value over a paper protocol is untested.
