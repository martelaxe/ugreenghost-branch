# Three directions after prior-art checks

The rankings below are choices for exploration, not proof of novelty or user value. We reject the proposed auralization and AAC-board experiences because their core interactions already exist. See [prior art](prior-art.md) and [all nine candidates](ideas.md).

## 1. Strangest potentially valuable: shared hearing/touch comparison

Two people receive different sensory representations of the same pair of stored sound segments. One uses a supported tactile interface; the other listens. They independently choose whether the rhythms match, then the system reveals the sources and uncertainty. The interesting interaction is an **asymmetric joint sensory task**, not a claim that vibration reproduces hearing. An independently designed haptic interface and participant choice are prerequisites.

This differs from Experiments 10, 17 and 18: it is not an archive-derived text puzzle or visual exhibit, and a person’s actual tactile interaction changes the outcome. It still overlaps existing haptic music games and accessibility tools; we did not find the exact combination in a limited search. No user demand or accessibility benefit has been tested. It requires feature extraction, haptic hardware, time alignment, synchronized independent sessions and reliable concealment; none is documented as a UGREEN Skill capability. That makes it a wild long-term investigation, not a realistic current entry.

## 2. Strongest unusual plausible UGREEN demonstration: qualified blind listening

A person asks whether two authorized recordings sound different. A system checks recorded capture context, hides identity, asks the person to listen and choose, then reveals both original sources and limitations. A second pair with missing microphone details is held back. The actual [local prototype](../../experiments/cross-domain-invention/prototype/index.html) performs this interaction with synthetic sounds and a rule-based gate; it has no AI or NAS connection.

Compared with 01–19, a human response and actual sound playback are the observable outcome; 19 reconstructed past facts, while this creates a new observation. Existing ABX tests and ecoacoustics platforms already do substantial portions, so the novel-combination claim rests on making capture-evidence prerequisites shape the interaction, which remains unvalidated. Its strongest platform path is a **conditional, partly manual demonstration**: UGREEN experts locate and read user-authorized capture notes; a separate human-controlled player performs masking and listening; the assistant reports what was actually supplied and what remains unknown. Whether UGREEN can choose and play anonymous audio itself without revealing identifiers is unknown. A Markdown Skill alone does not supply the browser player, true masking, deterministic gate or enforced blindness. If those are essential to the pitch, the platform cannot yet support it.

## 3. Biggest surprise from an underexplored field: blind physical experiment

In classroom science, a person can use stored photographs and capture rules to stage an actual coded comparison: two cardboard bridge designs are coded before one person loads them; the mapping is revealed after both outcomes. The user experiences the difference between observing a result and knowing which prior belief to associate with it. Blinding and experimental controls are longstanding, not our invention.

This differs from Experiment 17's One-Bit Workshop only if independent people can commit measurements before the code is revealed and the stored evidence tracks the same physical objects. Without that enforcement it is largely another guided workshop and should be rejected. UGREEN might retrieve user-authorized notes and ask one question at a time, but cannot guarantee blind allocation, separate-user access or valid measurement. A teacher with paper cards may do better. It is a field insight, not yet a separate compelling hackathon entry.

## The necessary decision

Of these, blind listening is the only one with an actual local demonstration here, and even it is probably a weak entry if forced into a text-only Skill. No real NAS, audio user, disability community or scientific collaborator validated the three interactions. The next honest test is a real-person session with consented recordings and deliberately incomplete capture data, paired with a fair existing ABX-tool-plus-checklist baseline. Do not infer improvement from the local demo alone.
