# Agent Skills frontier — checked 27 October 2026

Sources below are papers or authors' descriptions. Reported numbers are their experiments, not ours. Preprints are not all independently validated. A natural-language procedure can specify branches, checks and delegation, but execution is probabilistic and tool/harness dependent; deterministic enforcement and model-internal feedback require more than Markdown.

1. **SkillsBench: Benchmarking How Well Agent Skills Work Across Diverse Tasks**, Xiangyi Li et al., submitted 13 February, revised 14 June 2026, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12670). Their current 87-task, 18-configuration matched evaluation reports 33.9% → 50.5% with curated skills; focused ≤3-module bundles beat larger bundles and some smaller assisted models match larger unassisted models. Mechanism: curated reusable procedural knowledge plus task tools and deterministic verifiers, not weight updates; task-dependent and no guarantee on UGREEN. NAS idea: a tiny stateful procedure for adapting an exhibit, with a testable output, rather than an ever-growing archive manual.
2. **Skill-Use: Can LLMs Actually Use Skills in Agentic Harnesses?**, Jinyi Han et al., 5 August 2026, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04828). 79 skills, 177 Docker tasks and two harnesses separate trigger, compliance and boundary; best reported combined score 0.613. Trigger and compliance are distinct bottlenecks and model ranking changes across harnesses. Requires executable harness and trajectory checks. NAS idea: make activation and failed boundaries visible in a real-device test; merely pasting instructions into Codex proves neither triggering nor UGREEN compliance.
3. **Rep2Skill: Representation-Guided Skill Self-Evolution for LLM Agents**, Kaixing Zhang et al., 30 September; revised 13 October 2026, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39149). Authors compare internal activation trajectories of rollouts to identify deviation points and generate textual skill revisions; their two environments/two open models outperform text-only evolution. Requires model internals, rollout infrastructure and optimization; cannot be implemented by a Markdown NAS Skill. NAS speculation: a review-only local procedure laboratory that proposes revisions, if an exposed model supported the instrumentation.
4. **SkillCAT: Contrastive, Assessment-Augmented and Topology-Aware Skill Self-Evolution for LLM Agents**, Kunfeng Chen et al., 11 June; revised 29 July 2026, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13317). Contrasts successful/failed trajectories, replays candidate patches before admission, then routes relevant subskills. Authors report up to 49.69% average score improvement in their settings. Requires multi-run replay/evaluator and library infrastructure. NAS application: a stored policy is a *candidate*, not a trustworthy self-updating rule; owner approves tested revisions.
5. **Dynamic Agent Skills: A Lifecycle Survey and Taxonomy of Evolving Skill Libraries**, Yubo Li, 11 July 2026, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10113). A survey, not a new empirical procedure: 124-paper audit distinguishes collection, proposal, admission, retrieval, repair and governance. Files can hold procedures and data, but that does not supply validity, isolation or rollback. NAS idea: a visible quarantine shelf for unverified proposed procedures; actual isolation needs platform controls.
6. **A Survey on Agent Skills: Externalized Procedural Knowledge in Language Models**, Sanket Badhe, Priyanka Tiwari, Deep Shah and Nehal Kathrotia, 2026, [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/a-survey-on-agent-skills-externalized-procedural-knowledge-in-language-models/). Synthesizes modular skill lifecycle, routing, composition, security and adaptation. It is a conceptual survey, not proof any particular skill is reliable. NAS idea: treat changing rules as an inspectable artifact with provenance, without assuming they automatically become executable or enforceable.
7. **SKILLFOUNDRY: Building Self-Evolving Agent Skill Libraries from Heterogeneous Scientific Resources**, Shuaike Shen et al., 5 April 2026, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03964). Mines heterogeneous resources into contracts and tested skill packages; authors report gains on 5/6 benchmark datasets and two genomics tasks. Uses external resource ingestion and closed-loop code/runtime; not simple prompting. NAS application: derive a proposed human-guided household procedure from records, but validate it rather than blindly learning a routine.
8. **Proposer-Agent-Evaluator: Autonomous Skill Discovery for Foundation Model Internet Agents**, Yifei Zhou et al., ICML 2025, [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhou25ah.html). Proposes, executes and evaluates tasks and uses feedback for RL, reporting ~50% relative zero-shot improvement in its web tasks. It needs repeated interactions, an evaluator, and model training—not merely a SKILL.md. NAS idea: local synthetic rehearsals with separate checking, explicitly without claiming the system learned or changed its weights.

**Synthesis:** Skill content can describe a decision procedure and ask experts for evidence; dependable state transitions, safety, measurements and continuous updates need a runtime. Model strength, harness, source availability and skill triggering must all be separately tested. A weaker model benefit in SkillsBench is not evidence that our local UGREEN model can execute a particular idea.

9. **Rational Clarification by Assistive Agents via Value-of-Information Reasoning**, T. Duy Nguyen-Hien, Yee Whye Teh, Wee Sun Lee and Tan Zhi-Xuan, 29 September 2026, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37588). REVOIR estimates whether the answer to a question changes downstream reward rather than merely reducing uncertainty; the authors report fewer questions and stronger preference satisfaction on their two tasks without training. It is a closely related method, not a novel question-asking primitive invented here. Our tiny prompt does not implement its formal value model.
10. **"Do Not Mention This to the User": Detecting and Understanding Malicious Agent Skills in the Wild**, Yi Liu et al., 6 February, revised 10 June 2026, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06547). The authors report 157 behaviorally confirmed malicious skills from a 98,380-skill sample and examine credential theft and instruction manipulation. It used static plus dynamic detection; the numbers characterize that sample, not all skills. A NAS skill library must treat untrusted stored instructions as data and needs runtime isolation, not just a clever prompt.
11. **Scan the Skill, Govern the Action: Composing Registry Verdicts with Runtime Consequence Control**, Rohit Taneja and Travis Weber, 10 September 2026, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12001). Authors study registry verdicts and live-agent commands and propose a deterministic policy resolver. Their result motivates separating whether an instruction is installed from whether its current action is authorized. The resolver and trust ledger are infrastructure, not features we implemented.
