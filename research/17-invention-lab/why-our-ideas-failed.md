# Why the previous ideas did not yet feel like inventions

Experiments 01–02 produced grounded synthetic discoveries. Experiments 03 and 05 exposed a harder question: generic Codex given the same files can find the same thing; specialized verification even discarded candidates when it returned empty. There is no observed general intelligence gain. Experiment 06 repaired specific evidence-handling failures, not discovery intelligence.

Surprise Me, Missing Piece and Creative Salvage asked for useful connections or new directions. They produced mainly text; a competent file-aware assistant could often produce similar findings. Experiment 09's small scoring advantage was unreliable and citations/coverage failed. Impact Rehearsal highlighted consequences but also resembled ordinary analysis; Project Rescue yielded a tangible *local* copy, yet the generic baseline selected it too and actual NAS safe copy remains unverified. Copies are not a new kind of storage by themselves.

Task Capsule and Task Continuity stored working context. That is useful, but saved Markdown handoffs and existing agents already support closely related behavior; generic comparisons did not prove a distinctive workflow. Semantic Readiness / Readiness Delta made a meaningful pass-to-fail change visible, but again both methods did so; citation links and file counts failed. These are judgments about experiments, not proof the user problems do not exist.

The recurring mistake was crediting AI reasoning, a polished name or NAS locality as a new primitive. The next invention needs an observable operation: an object or interaction changes under a user-selected condition, with explicit state, constraints and failure. But a real new operation may require a runtime that a Markdown Skill cannot supply. A scripted projection or a chat imitation must not be misrepresented as that runtime.
