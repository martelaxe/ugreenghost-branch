# Reconstruction rules

## 1. Establish whether sources belong together

Compare actual dish names, explicit recipe identifiers, dates if supported, ingredients and family context. Similar filenames, visual appearance or a matching first name are clues, not proof. Label uncertain connections and ask the user rather than combining different dishes. Treat an attributed speaker or family relationship as confirmed only if evidence or the owner identifies them.

## 2. Preserve source differences

Build a source ledger for each ingredient, quantity, preparation step and relevant condition: source path or stable identifier actually returned; excerpt or time reference only if actually available; the content and its uncertainty. Do not manufacture quotes or timestamps. Mark low-confidence OCR or audio as unconfirmed; request a readable card, audio check or owner clarification. Distinguish a family memory from a direct instruction to prepare the food.

- Agreement across sources can support a shared detail but cannot prove authenticity.
- Different values: show alternatives beside their respective sources. Date alone does not prove one is the definitive or latest recipe. Ask which version the family intends if needed.
- A missing value is unknown, not zero. Do not fill missing cooking instructions, temperatures, durations, quantities or safety information from assumptions or general knowledge.
- If a source is absent, inaccessible, unreadable, truncated or unsupported, identify it as unexamined. Do not infer it does not exist or that the archive is complete.
- Do not treat a predicted dish in a photo as proof of its ingredients, safe preparation or identity. Separate appearance descriptions from recipe evidence.

## 3. Useful result despite incomplete material

Produce an honest family draft that preserves the supported parts and makes unknowns actionable: for example, “Card A says one cup milk; transcript B says one cup water. Ask which version you want.” If essential instructions remain unresolved, call it **incomplete—confirm before cooking**. Do not claim allergy safety, storage safety, food safety or suitability for any person. Users should independently verify those details. Avoid adding unrelated safety warnings when the question is archival only.

## 4. Story and source-based collection

A short story can summarize actually supported family context, labeled as a memory rather than an independently verified historical fact. Do not embellish or infer private family facts. A photo album is optional; a photo can be included as a family-approved related image without asserting it depicts the exact dish or event. Ask before any saving or album creation and preserve originals.
