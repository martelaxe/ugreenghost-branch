# Cross-domain invention — decision

**Recommendation: pursue one small, serious prototype of a capture-qualified blind listening workflow.** Its distinctive claim is not that audio comparison is new: it is that an archive can decide whether a comparison is ready to run, create a brief human listening activity, and reveal the source conditions after the person's choice. This is a **possible combination**, not proven novelty. The current local implementation is a simple rules-and-audio demo with fictional fixtures, not AI or NAS software. More importantly, the defining blind player and enforced gate are **not documented UGREEN Skill primitives**; a Markdown-only submission would currently retain only a partial, mostly conversational version. A serious continuation is justified only as a short falsification test, not a competition commitment.

## What was explored

Nine interactions span citizen science, accessibility, music and performance, physical-science education, shared privacy, acoustics and community observation. The cross-domain search deliberately rejected appealing duplicates. A university [auralization demo](https://mta.hmu.gr/en/research/in-brief-research/research-demos/auralisation/) already lets people hear uploaded audio in measured spaces; [MyTalkTools](https://www.mytalktools.com/dnn/prod/home.html) already pairs custom images and recorded speech; [TCFS](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/events/usenix01/freenix01/full_papers/cattaneo/cattaneo_html/node10.html) already describes threshold sharing. None becomes distinctive simply by adding a NAS.

The leading three proposals are:

1. **Strangest:** an asymmetric two-person comparison in which one person hears and another feels a stored sound's rhythm, and they independently respond before reveal. This is distinct from our previous text puzzles but overlaps [audio-to-tactile](https://github.com/google/audio-to-tactile) and [haptic rhythm games](https://charm.stanford.edu/ME327/2023-Group2). It needs custom hardware, separate synchronized interfaces and design with disabled users; it is not a UGREEN Skill today.
2. **Most plausible partial UGREEN demonstration:** blind listening conditioned on capture evidence. UGREEN experts could plausibly retrieve selected records and present known/missing conditions; a separate human-controlled player would have to perform the blind interaction unless device testing proves otherwise. Exact media identifiers, output visibility and playback are unverified.
3. **Unexpected field:** use experimental blinding for a classroom physical test, such as comparing two coded cardboard bridges. The method itself is old. Unless the interaction creates genuinely independent commitments and captures object-linked evidence, it is too close to Experiment 17's One-Bit Workshop and a teacher with index cards.

More detail and explicit differences from Experiments 01–19 are in [final three](../research/cross-domain-invention/final-three.md).

## Observable proof built here

[Open the local demonstration](../experiments/cross-domain-invention/prototype/index.html). It gives the user two generated four-second tone recordings in randomized order, asks which contains more tones, and reveals their labels after a choice. Selecting a different fictional pair with an unknown microphone blocks listening and reports what is missing. The files, generator, metadata, rules, interface and hashes are preserved in [the prototype folder](../experiments/cross-domain-invention/prototype/README.md).

This demonstrates a real local **state change and gate** in code and creates real audio files. The rule engine passed 11 checks; a mocked-interface test verified blocking and reveal transitions; WAV headers and hashes matched. The mocked audio object did not play sound, and actual browser playback was not verified. The interface can tell that both browser playback requests started, not that anyone heard the clips. Developer tools expose the fixture mapping, so this is casual masking, not secure experimental blindness. No ecological conclusion, user feedback, real capture metadata, AI inference or UGREEN runtime result exists. See [test results](../experiments/cross-domain-invention/prototype/test-results.md).

## Competition decision, evaluated separately

- **Originality:** The proposed interaction may combine evidence-gated preparation with an actual blind activity, rather than producing a document summary. [ABX listening tools](https://github.com/jaakkopasanen/ABX), audio test interfaces and ecological study protocols already implement most ingredients. We did not locate this exact consumer workflow in a short targeted search; that is not proof it does not exist. Generic AI plus an ABX tool and checklist may be just as good.
- **Competing products:** [Open Ecoacoustics](https://openecoacoustics.org/) already supports ecological audio research, and [bird survey guidance](https://birdsurveyguidelines.org/acoustic-survey-methods/) explicitly records capture conditions. Scientific systems can already conduct before/after studies. The prototype is dramatically less capable than these alternatives.
- **Immediate WOW:** Hearing two recordings blind and revealing a missing-capture blocker could be more engaging than a long report. It may also feel like an ordinary A/B test. No judge or user has seen or rated this demo.
- **User value:** If the system prevents a false conclusion and makes a curiosity-driven comparison easy, it could help an amateur or class. No evidence shows that people would choose it over a normal audio player, checklist or existing science tool. Pairing settings alone cannot establish comparability or causality.
- **UGREEN minimum:** Read authorized files and actual source descriptions; resolve usable, authorized audio items; retain the candidate/condition mapping while keeping it hidden from the listening person; produce two clips; receive their response; expose source and caveats; stop when prerequisites are unknown. `file-agent`, `knowledge-agent`/`document-agent` and `media-agent` document relevant fragments, but parent-visible audio results, exact concealment, player behavior and state continuity are unknown. None were tested on hardware.
- **Markdown reduction:** A Skill can propose a checklist and ask the user to perform a manual comparison. It cannot itself guarantee hidden metadata, random allocation, secure multi-user independence, a graphical player, a program-enforced gate or signal processing. Therefore the central sensory experience does **not currently survive intact** as a documented Markdown-only Skill. A possible hybrid demo with a separate player should not be represented as a platform-native capability or final compliant submission.

## Next serious prototype: one falsifiable comparison

Use two consented real short recordings and a truthful capture log, including at least one pair with an unknown microphone or different gain. Ask one independent person to perform the same task twice: once using an existing ABX player plus a short capture checklist, once with the proposed workflow. Counterbalance order and avoid revealing labels; preserve source conditions and observer feedback. Check if they detect the missing context, can actually hear the comparison, and find the extra workflow valuable. Do not use one answer as evidence of ecological change. This tests user value and prior-art overlap; it still would not establish scientific accuracy or UGREEN execution. Separately, when hardware is available, test whether a delegated media item can be played without revealing its identity in the UI. If either the user interaction is ordinary or hiding cannot be demonstrated, stop or abandon this direction.
