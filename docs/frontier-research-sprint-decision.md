# Frontier research sprint — decision

Research cutoff: **October 27, 2026**. This is research only: no new Skill, device run, user study or prototype was created. **Decision: test a specific cross-media interaction next, while keeping an ambitious synchronized-video experience and a grounded migration check as alternatives.** None of the three has demonstrated superiority to generic AI or existing products. The wildest is not feasible with the documented UGREEN operations alone.

## What changed

We did not exhaust the idea space in Experiments 01–19. We repeatedly tested text reasoning, archive interpretation, photo selection and task handoff, mostly on small synthetic archives. Those experiments usefully exposed generic-agent parity, brittle evidence handling and unverified runtime contracts; they did not test enough real audio, time-aligned video, media migration or actual household needs. The [coverage map](../research/frontier-sprint/coverage-audit.md) distinguishes deep coverage from underexplored work.

The most useful new-to-this-project mechanisms are: (1) validated explicit execution state rather than growing transcripts, in [SKILL.state](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26263); (2) prerequisite-aware skill retrieval in [Graph-of-Skills](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05333); (3) keeping a skill fixed while changing how it is exposed, in [SkillAlign](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07255); (4) executable intervention functions in [HASP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17734); (5) aligning evidence to an event *before* retrieval in [EM²Mem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00551); (6) audio-waveform alignment of separately recorded media in [SyncSink](https://github.com/JorenSix/SyncSink); and (7) optimizing erasure-code repair around disk access and seek costs in [LESS](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast26/presentation/cheng). These are published mechanisms, not capabilities we have implemented or a claim that they are new globally. See the [source-by-source review](../research/frontier-sprint/research-discoveries.md) for evidence and limits.

A striking user problem: a migrated photo library may retain the still image while losing the motion, or even possess a paired motion file that the destination does not play. An [Immich missing-component report](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/issues/22601) and a separate [paired-but-not-playing report](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/issues/30751) illustrate different failures; they are individual reports, not a market-size estimate. Other promising areas are navigating spoken archives and explaining what camera evidence is actually available; the [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/programs/veterans-history-project/how-to-participate/transcribing-interviews/) describes significant manual oral-history transcription effort, and [Frigate](https://docs.frigate.video/usage/history/) documents multiple reasons footage may be missing. Neither establishes consumer demand for our specific solution.

UGREEN's installed catalog documents experts for audio transcription, media search/playback, camera events/evidence and photo/file metadata. We underexplored these. It does **not** establish a waveform synchronizer, synchronized player, video editor, system-health API, guaranteed transcript timestamps or Live Photo pairing metadata. A catalog entry is not proof the parent receives a useful result or that a device works offline. See [exact operations and approval boundaries](../research/frontier-sprint/ugreen-unexplored-capabilities.md).

## Exactly three directions

### Wild — The same minute, many cameras

**Idea:** Turn separately recorded clips from an occasion into a shared-moment view. The viewer switches from one person's camera to another without losing the moment; common audio estimates relative time despite wrong clocks.

**60-second demonstration concept:** Show three actual recordings of the same birthday, each with different timestamps. Ask “Show the candle moment from everyone.” A synchronized player opens on one clip; switch twice while the song continues in time. Reveal the estimated alignment and flag a clip that cannot be reliably synchronized. This is a proposed experience, not something built in this sprint.

**Honest competition and feasibility:** [Final Cut Pro](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/guide/final-cut-pro/ver8798d940/mac) already synchronizes multicam clips using waveform audio; [SyncSink](https://github.com/JorenSix/SyncSink) synchronizes recordings; a [patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8111326B1/en) describes closely related behavior. Potential differentiation is a simple, consent-aware household NAS experience for finding and viewing already-stored recordings, not new synchronization technology. UGREEN's catalog provides media search but not audio decoding/alignment or a synchronized viewing UI. Without additional infrastructure this cannot be the advertised hackathon Skill. Compared with Experiments 18–19, the result would be an interactive temporal media experience rather than a static curation or historical evidence analysis; neither novelty nor usefulness is established.

### Bridge — Hear the picture

**Idea:** Find a recorded explanation of a particular physical object or handwritten item, and present a defensible connection between the recording and an actual photo. For example, an archived voice recording names a recipe and two ingredients that also appear on a selected recipe card.

**60-second demonstration concept:** Show a real handwritten recipe photo and ask, “Do we have a recording explaining this?” Show a real audio file, a transcript excerpt and the card side by side; highlight two independent matching details and listen to the original if the platform genuinely supports it. Then show a similar wrong card and explain the mismatch. If the audio is too vague, the output says **possible match, please confirm**. Do not invent a seek time or imply a recording is about a card merely because it mentions a common ingredient.

**Honest competition and feasibility:** [OHMS](https://journal.code4lib.org/articles/10643) already synchronizes searchable oral-history transcripts to original audio; [Descript](https://www.descript.com/tools/audio-text) synchronizes transcripts and media; [Cinema Memory](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/projects/cmda/searcharchive/) links interview collections and memorabilia. We propose linking independently stored photo and speech sources at request time, but that might be ordinary multimodal search or already available elsewhere. UGREEN documents `knowledge-agent` audio transcription and `photo-agent` OCR, and `file-agent` source discovery. Parent-visible excerpts, exact timecodes, cross-modal matching and playback are unverified; this direction is feasible only as a **qualified pairing of actual returned sources**, not the full synchronized interaction. It overlaps Experiment 07's conversation bridge and 18's photo display but tests a previously underexplored audio-image connection. Generic Codex may do it equally well.

### Practical wildcard — Live Photo survival check

**Idea:** Before retiring an old library, compare explicitly identified source/destination components of selected moving photos and reveal which still, motion or sidecar components have actual supporting evidence in the checked locations.

**60-second demonstration concept:** Show a moving photo on the old device, then show a migration folder containing only the still. Ask, “Did this moving photo survive?” Display one row: **still found and byte-matched; motion not found in selected destination; playback unverified**. A second row shows both components found but playback unknown. Provide paths and scope, with no automatic deletion, copying or claims about content outside the checked folders.

**Honest competition and feasibility:** [Immich](https://docs.immich.app/features/xmp-sidecars/) and [PhotoPrism](https://docs.photoprism.app/getting-started/faq/) already handle metadata and sidecars; [KitForma PhotoProof](https://photoproof.kitforma.com/) advertises a local source-to-destination byte comparison and Live Photo component guidance, and [multi-component transfer prior art](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20250335400A1/en) exists. PhotoProof is an especially close competitor; its publication date and performance are unverified. Pairing and playback may be app-specific. UGREEN documents bounded file listing, hashes and photo metadata, but not guaranteed pair identifiers or destination-app playback verification. A sensible minimal test needs user-supplied known pair mappings and explicit caveats. Unlike Project Rescue in Experiment 16, this checks the survival of a composite representation rather than choosing one source to restore; nonetheless both are file-integrity workflows, and no distinct advantage has been proved.

## Strategy and next step

Stop repeating broad AI-NAS searches, prompt-only ‘surprise’ concepts, and synthetic text benchmarks that again show competent generic file reasoning. Keep targeted prior-art checks and small tests where a result can fail. Consider user benefit and an observable experience before the Skill architecture.

**The one next tiny prototype to consider—not launched here:** use one consented or public-domain real short audio recording and two real images, one containing a specific corroborated label and one misleadingly similar. Have an independent person specify the correct connection and whether evidence supports it, then give an ordinary agent and a narrowly guided agent the same transcript, images and exact source access. Compare whether each identifies the right original, rejects or qualifies the decoy, cites an exact excerpt, and abstains when the decisive audio phrase is removed. Record whether a person actually finds the pairing helpful. If neither model can do better than generic retrieval, users find it ordinary, or a workable UGREEN handoff cannot return the needed sources, stop. Run a separately scoped hardware check of actual transcription and OCR outputs if device access becomes available; do not fabricate an expert envelope or assume transcription timestamps. This small proposed test is not a claim of runtime capability or statistically significant advantage.
