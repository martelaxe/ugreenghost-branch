# Experiment 10 — UGREEN WOW tournament decision

**Provisional primary: Archive Quest. Fallback: Pocket Exhibit.** This is a choice of which distinct experience to investigate, **not a claim that the current prototype is exceptional**. The actual puzzle is a simple year-and-arithmetic exercise, its answer appears in the same response, two clues were misrepresented as exact quotations, and no person has played it. Source-based escape rooms already exist. The concept would need a much better, accurate game and positive independent user feedback before we should pitch it as a standout submission.

We reviewed the key lessons from Experiments 01–09: generic Codex found a potential deposit issue and cross-file opportunities; specialized prompts or harnesses have not shown a reliable general reasoning advantage; evidence and source coverage can be lost; a Markdown Skill passed static validation but real expert returns remain unverified; Creative Salvage's useful alternatives and rights checking substantially overlap competitors and generic AI. This tournament shifts attention to **what the user receives**, not a claim of superior intelligence.

## The four actual local results

All four are one-shot results from fresh local Codex processes, each given a separate authorized 18-file fictional text archive. Full prompts, events, raw reports, source fixtures, hashes and audits are in [experiment 10](../experiments/10-wow-tournament/). No UGREEN NAS agent ran.

1. **Archive Quest** — An old ticket, diary and stock notes become a playable text puzzle. The player identifies the 2019 lantern color and subtracts sheets used; the marked solution is **BLUE 12**. [Actual output](../experiments/10-wow-tournament/outputs/quest/raw-response.md). **WOW hypothesis:** a personal archive becomes a game people share. **Useful result:** a short activity with source paths, possibly a conversation starter. **Reality check:** nobody played it; the clues are obvious and the answer appears directly below the game. Two supposedly verbatim quotations were slightly changed. This exact game is not ready for demonstration.
2. **Pocket Exhibit** — Field notes and an abandoned paper workshop become a six-panel text-only zine, with source facts, separately labeled invented verse and a photo-use restriction. [Actual output](../experiments/10-wow-tournament/outputs/alchemy/raw-response.md). **Useful result:** editable text for a small educational zine. It is not a rendered or printed object, and its creative merit is untested.
3. **Digital Archaeologist** — An event contract, deposit payment and final invoice suggest a **possible** $420 unapplied or unreturned deposit. The output drafts a message asking the venue to clarify. [Actual output](../experiments/10-wow-tournament/outputs/archaeologist/raw-response.md). **Useful result:** a cautious inquiry; no refund or payment error is established and no message was sent.
4. **Fork in the Budget** — A $120 current quote and $200 proposed revision produce conditional totals of $200 and $280 against a $240 ceiling. [Actual output](../experiments/10-wow-tournament/outputs/fork/raw-response.md). **Useful result:** two clearly labeled budget drafts and a decision, with the revision still unapproved.

The [12-idea field](../research/10-wow-tournament/wow-ideas.md), [90-second storyboards](../research/10-wow-tournament/demo-storyboards.md), and [ranking and hard checks](../research/10-wow-tournament/wow-ranking.md) describe each concept separately. Archive Quest is new **to this project**, not globally new. Its selection emphasizes the requested memorability and emotional interaction over the unweighted score: the author gave Pocket Exhibit and Archaeologist 18/25 each, Quest 17/25, Fork 15/25. These ratings are subjective, uncertain and not user feedback.

## Why the provisional winner could be compelling

A good Archive Quest would turn an authorized collection into a short, solvable shared experience: players discover a personally meaningful fact through source-backed clues, then review the original files. The NAS supplies stored material and a familiar place for a family or team to collect it. This is a product hypothesis: a generic assistant with the same uploaded or mounted files can also create the game, as this local run demonstrates. No privacy/local-model guarantee follows from NAS storage.

The comparison is serious: [Escaply](https://www.escaply.com/en) advertises generating escape rooms from source material, [docs-to-quiz](https://docs2quiz.github.io/getting-started/introduction/) describes source-cited question generation, and [Family Game Night](https://familygamenight.app/) advertises personalized family games. These are product claims, not hands-on verification. The proposed distinction is a user-authorized personal archive and a source reveal, but it is not established that competitors cannot offer that. If the judges see only the current color-and-count exercise, the concept may look like an ordinary AI quiz.

## Reliability and feasibility

The [evidence audit](../experiments/10-wow-tournament/evidence-checks.md) found all 25 unique source filenames cited in inline code exist in the respective fictional fixtures. That checks paths only, not complete citation coverage or semantic truth. The Quest answer is supported, but two presented quotes are paraphrased rather than exact. All four reports cite paths relative to a temporary investigator workspace; they are not directly clickable from the saved report, and must be resolved against the corresponding fixture folder. The zine explicitly labels new writing and excludes a restricted photo. The financial issue remains a possibility, and budget revisions remain conditional.

The official installed UGREEN catalog documents read-only `file-agent` discovery, `document-agent` extraction/reconciliation, and `knowledge-agent` evidence collection, with optional `photo-agent` search/vision. The parent must delegate to registered experts and handle completion, failure, cancellation and waiting states; it cannot assume a custom UI, generated video, background service or its own direct CLI access. These are documented building blocks only. Actual NAS file formats, retrievable source IDs/excerpts, permission scope, partial-result coverage, ability to hide a spoiler, and user-answer handling across turns are unverified. The first version could be a **single static text puzzle plus separate source/solution text**, without claiming live interactive play; any saved file needs the documented approval workflow.

The investigators ran in separate mount-isolated workspaces with project and answer key hidden at their ordinary paths, but other host paths and Codex runtime directories remained visible, and the same backend/user was shared. No formal proof of total isolation exists. All 18 files per case were fictional, source hashes were unchanged, and all four processes completed. One author-created synthetic sample per idea is not proof of real-world surprise, user value or general reliability.

## Next step

**Get real reactions before building a new Skill.** Use the prepared [anonymized packet](../experiments/10-wow-tournament/human-packet/participant-packet.md) and [feedback protocol](../research/10-wow-tournament/human-feedback-protocol.md) with 3–5 consenting people. Ask which makes them most excited to own an AI NAS, allowing “none”; ask them to solve the game and explain what feels generic. Excerpts differ in length, so treat responses as exploratory, not a fair quantitative ranking. In parallel, verify on actual UGREEN hardware or with organizer-provided real expert output whether source references and a separate answer-reveal interaction can be supported. Do not present local output as device footage.

Continue Quest only if people actually enjoy a more meaningful puzzle, quotes are exact, and a safe scoped text flow is plausible. If participants favor a practical result, the source-grounded text zine is the fallback. If neither is compelling, return to ideation rather than forcing a winner.
