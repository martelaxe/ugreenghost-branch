# Experiment 16 — Project Rescue decision

**Keep Project Rescue as a conditional practical Round 1 review candidate, not a proven Most Creative contender.** It can identify a missing project's intended file by linking review, version and export records, then propose a source-preserving copy. In two small fictional domains, the actual frozen Skill-guided Codex and a competent generic Codex both found the intended candidate and handled tested negative cases. A separate local demonstration copied the chosen SVG and verified unchanged originals. This is not UGREEN execution. The major unresolved issue is whether UGREEN can perform the exact approved copy with atomic no-overwrite; if not established, the Skill remains in proposal mode.

## What was built and what passed

- A new [Markdown-only Skill](../prototype/project-rescue-v2/SKILL.md), preserving the original, delegates file operations and document analysis to their registered experts. It uses actual returned evidence, asks for scoped read authorization and separate exact-action approval, and distinguishes pending, failed and completed outcomes. It asks for evidence of atomic no-overwrite before attempting a copy; `overwrite=false` plus a prior absence check is not a guarantee. It does not claim to undelete bytes, fully map dependencies, or repair an application.
- The official installed checker returned `static-pass`, **0 errors and 0 warnings**, SHA-256 `6e433eb4facdb777de466f99bdab4666596aac39aedb5a6bb2f7d43f4c2f4371`. See [checker output](../experiments/16-project-rescue/static-check.json). Static success does not validate runtime.
- Two distinct **25-file** fictional archives tested identification without an expected hash: the creative candidate `shared/q7.svg` is a real vector asset linked to the approved version by separate records, and the business candidate `retained/x3.txt` matches the accepted record while external confirmation remains unknown. Both G and S selected the supported source and exact target, cited the critical chain, retained shared copies, and sought approval. The fixtures are deliberately well organized and do not establish authenticity or realistic performance in arbitrary archives.
- Fourteen fresh local Codex processes covered two domains plus ambiguous identity, restricted scope, occupied target, fictional failed verification and overt prompt injection, for G and S. Both abstained where appropriate. The agents were read-only and no NAS expert was invoked. The [full comparison](../experiments/16-project-rescue/evaluation.md) preserves exact prompts, responses, logs and workspaces.
- The [local demo](../experiments/16-project-rescue/demo/before-after.md) took the model-proposed source and target, then used Python exclusive creation with synthetic owner and gateway flags on a disposable clone. Source before/after and destination hashes matched, and all original files were unchanged. Seven deterministic adapter checks passed, including collision, changed source, pending approvals and a post-copy injected verification failure that correctly reports **unverified**. These are local research tests, not UGREEN gateway tests.

## What failed or remains weak

Two Skill-guided reports claimed 26 files in 25-file workspaces. Their raw Markdown references also use an ephemeral `/tmp/work/` mount and do not resolve from their saved report location. The saved fixture paths permit auditing; that is not a portable citation feature. One trial per cell and author-created data do not establish accuracy, reliability or efficiency generally. Generic Codex matched the core results, and no real users judged usefulness or surprise. The strongest demo is a staged chain of honest evidence, but a careful person can follow the same records; no time saving was measured.

Three fresh simulated reviewers, given the actual request, model outputs, selected source evidence, copy record and competitor limitations, scored normalized 47, 50 and 53/100. Their innovation scores were 7, 7 and 9/25. These are model opinions, not actual judge scores or winning probabilities; their packet omitted some original source files and the copy program. They liked the shared-source reveal but cited generic parity and unverified NAS behavior. See [judge review](../research/16-project-rescue/judge-review.md).

Adobe Premiere can search and relink missing media; Windows File History, Time Machine and Synology snapshots recover backed-up versions; UGREEN advertises semantic search and file chat. Project Rescue is a proposed combination of contextual identity reasoning and an approval-gated source-preserving copy, not a demonstrated unique technology or replacement for backups. See [competitive red team](../research/16-project-rescue/competitive-red-team.md).

## The gates

**A — Technical credibility: conditional.** Registered agent ownership and possible operations are documented; destination interpretation, atomic collision handling, real gateway approval/resume, source/target hash propagation and readback are unknown. The Skill must stop if safety cannot be established. The finalist action is plausible, not demonstrated.

**B — Generality: limited local planning pass.** Both domains and one ambiguous case worked without an expected checksum, but two count errors and synthetic fixtures limit the finding.

**C — Creative differentiation: not established.** Generic parity, significant competitor overlap and absent human validation mean the idea should be presented as a practical candidate, not advertised as a proven creative breakthrough. [Detailed gates](../research/16-project-rescue/decision-gates.md).
