# Experiment 18 — Living Archive decision

**WEAK.** Real photographs and visual model inputs made the experiment substantially stronger than Experiment 17. The narrative-guided curator visibly preserved a missing story beat as a gap, rather than inventing a shelter image; a generic curator instead rewrote the story around a real ruins photograph. Both were reasonable, and the generic curator made the same central choices in the second comparison. We have not shown a distinctive storage mechanism, an advantage in understanding, a real-user preference or a working UGREEN experience. The interactive vision remains interesting, but the demonstrated output is static local curation, not a living archive.

## Collection and original story

We used **15 real images** downloaded as Wikimedia Commons thumbnails, each with source metadata marked public domain, mainly of the 1906 San Francisco earthquake and fire. The collection also included a 1915 exhibition and a 1989 damaged bridge as potential decoys. It included one photo cataloged as a rescue and one cataloged as a refugee camp, as well as two separate reproductions of the same Palace Hotel fire photograph. [Attributions, creators, source links and hashes](../experiments/18-living-archive/fixtures/attribution.md) and the full metadata are retained. The individual Commons records supply the historical labels; the model also received the actual visual images on labelled contact sheets.

A fresh Codex process, unaware of the later changes, selected three panels: **the burning Palace Hotel → rescue in the ruins → Jefferson Square refugee camp**. It cited the photo catalog for the specific rescue and camp identifications and described the three pictures as thematic scenes, not a sequence of the same people. The [actual original response](../experiments/18-living-archive/original/raw-run/response.md) and [original visual](../experiments/18-living-archive/original/exhibit.png) are saved.

## What happened when evidence changed

- **A — Fire image removed:** the narrative-guided agent selected Image 04, a separate reproduction of the *same* photograph as the original Image 09. This keeps the Palace Hotel claim, but is a weak substitute test: it is not an independent photograph of another fire scene. Its “independent catalog record” language must not be read as independent photographic evidence. The collection did contain a different city-fire image, but the agent did not select it here.
- **B — Rescue image removed:** both the generic and guided curators chose Image 02, a tree and rubble, and **abandoned the rescue claim**. Both retitled the exhibit around fire, ruins and shelter. A visually attractive 1915 fair photo and a 1989 bridge photo remained in the pool; neither was presented as a 1906 rescue.
- **C — Refugee-camp image removed:** no other image was cataloged as a shelter scene. The guided curator left a visible gap and retained the general shelter need only as context. The generic curator chose the same ruins image 02, changed the third role, and retitled the story *Fire, Rescue, Ruins*—it did not claim the rubble depicted a camp. Both responses are supported and legitimate; the first exposes the missing evidence more directly.
- **D — Fictional use restriction:** both images 09 and 04 remained physically present, but an exercise-only rule excluded them from this public exhibit. The guided curator selected the separate city-fire Image 15 and dropped the specific Palace Hotel and April 18 image claim. This was a fictional policy constraint, **not a change to actual rights, access control or previously shared copies**.

[Before-and-after visual review](../experiments/18-living-archive/visual-review/index.html) and [overview PNG](../experiments/18-living-archive/visual-review/overview-original-vs-C.png) show the actual model selections, captions and gaps, rendered locally. One actual reasoning success is C: the guided curator did not equate a rubble photograph with proof of a shelter. One actual limitation is A: the apparently successful “replacement” is another scan of the same image, not proof that a different photograph preserved meaning. The model's description of dark smoke in low-contrast Image 15 is also an interpretation, and it relied on the catalog to identify the scene.

No obvious invented historical event or fictional-rights-as-real claim was found in a review of the central panel claims; this is not an exhaustive audit by a historian. The input context supported the general need for shelter but linked to a broad USGS page; the [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/places/000/1906-earthquake-cottages.htm) corroborates that claim **after the experiment**, and was not read by the models. The source titles do not establish identities or specific outcomes for the people photographed. [Detailed evaluation](../experiments/18-living-archive/evaluation.md).

## Did the narrative procedure add value?

For B and C, generic and guided agents got the same model, instructions to remain truthful, source files, image attachments, original exhibit and structured output format. N received additional explicit role-and-evidence tracking instructions. B produced the same source selection and meaningful reframing in both methods. In C, both avoided a false shelter substitution; N showed a gap while G changed the narrative. That difference is visible and could matter to an editor who wants the missing beat preserved, but **neither option has been shown to be better**. One run per method and condition cannot establish reliability, user value or superiority. The structured JSON request and local renderer helped make both agents' changes explicit; this is part of the research harness, not a proven Skill feature.

The [blind comparison packet](../experiments/18-living-archive/visual-review/blind/index.html) lets you compare anonymized outputs without treatment labels. No external participant was contacted. The four adaptation conditions were fixed before launch; the separate answer key was written after launch but before outputs were inspected, so no independent pre-launch key freeze can be claimed. The source images, model prompts, output, event logs and hash records are preserved. Seven fresh read-only model processes completed; the sandbox did not establish absolute isolation or network disconnection.

## Does this feel like new storage?

The **specific visible behavior**—a story retaining a blank for a lost source—is more concrete than ordinary file search. But a normal AI curator asked to update an exhibit can also rewrite or omit a panel; the generic baseline did so truthfully. Here, a local Python/Pillow script rendered each independent model result as a static image. There is no persistent narrative dependency system, automatic trigger, reactive visual exhibit or NAS-created artifact. The imagined living archive would need those mechanisms, reliable provenance, permissions and semantic checks. The present demo is mostly a careful curation workflow with a visible diff.

The strongest nearby systems include [Monet](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/monet-system-reliving-memories-theme-based-photo-storytelling/), which selects personal photos and remixes them into stories; [Collaposer](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791160), which prepares source-photo assets for visual storytelling; and the public UGREEN submission [Publish-Safe Photo Review](https://devpost.com/software/publish-safe-photo-review), which proposes eligibility-aware album selection. None of the reviewed descriptions establishes this exact missing-evidence comparison, but no global originality claim is justified. [Focused prior-art review](../research/18-living-archive/prior-art-review.md).

## UGREEN feasibility and next action

The installed official catalog documents file-agent discovery and reads, document-agent extraction and QA, photo-agent search and visual analysis, workflow-approved photo album creation, and workflow-approved knowledge-agent text saving. A **user-initiated textual plan** is plausible; an actual album depends on real identifiers, approval and device behavior. Neither guarantees a rendered three-panel visual, a stable dependency graph, automatic updates, correct rights judgments, ordering or revocation. The local renderer is not an allowed Markdown-only Skill component. [Capability assessment](../research/18-living-archive/ugreen-feasibility.md). No new Skill was built because the experiment did not show a compelling enough distinction.

**Decision: WEAK.** There is a valid visual moment, but it is not yet convincing evidence of a new kind of storage or a strong Most Creative entry. **Next action:** personally review the [blind comparison packet](../experiments/18-living-archive/visual-review/blind/index.html) and decide whether the visible gap is meaningfully preferable to the generic curator's honest reframing. Do not start another experiment automatically.
