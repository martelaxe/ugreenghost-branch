# Lessons learned

1. **The concept has an encouraging small-scale signal.** Both independent investigators linked an overlooked photo archive to an open cultural call, then described useful next steps. They cited the supporting files and did not promise a fee or confirmed selection.
2. **Rights changed the advice.** When only the permission clause changed, the second investigator flagged the restriction and suggested seeking written permission. This is a promising demonstration of distinguishing an attractive match from a cleared use, not proof of reliable legal analysis.
3. **Surprise needs evidence and context.** The strongest result combined the call, archive inventory and image index, license, studio records and capacity. The opportunity was plausible but not guaranteed, and both reports surfaced extra uncertainty about image dates, releases and printing.
4. **The current experiment is narrow.** The 22 documents are synthetic, text-only, short and English-language; the deliberately relevant call, index and license are relatively accessible. Two runs provide no stable success rate or proof of performance on messy folders, PDFs, photographs, missing or conflicting rights, or larger archives.
5. **Local Codex is not the NAS Skill.** No UGREEN agents, supported document extraction behavior, interface, or runtime were tested. A feasibility experiment using the official capability catalog and, when available, actual hardware is needed before promising the workflow.
6. **Next experiment:** build a held-out archive with a different kind of opportunity, one no-op archive, and mixed-strength or missing evidence; preregister expected outcomes. Use repeated independent investigators and measure supported discoveries, false positives, and missed opportunities. Keep another person or process responsible for generating hidden fixtures where possible.
