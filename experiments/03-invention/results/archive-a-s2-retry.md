## 1. Turn a retired schematic into a tabletop history discussion aid

### Proposed combination and usefulness

Combine the staff-owned WE-98 historic schematic and coordinate export with approved acrylic offcuts, the retained raised-layer technique, and lessons about high-contrast labels and spoken orientation. The result would be a **small internal sample for discussing historic spatial relationships**, potentially followed by a proposed-material review with the reading room.

The unexpected connection is between a dismantled exhibition’s spatial data, a volunteer craft technique, current fabrication capacity, and a newly expressed difficulty following group history discussions.

### Hypothesis, feasibility, and limits

**Hypothesis:** A sparse raised diagram, paired with high-contrast labels and spoken orientation, could make historic spatial discussion easier for some participants. No file demonstrates that benefit.

**Feasibility:** Moderate for a small internal sample, conditional on manager allocation and a credible four-hour work estimate. External use remains unestablished.

Important uncertainties and contrary evidence:

- The actual WE-98 drawing is listed on an archive drive in `02-archive/chart-register.csv`; it is not supplied here. The coordinate file contains points, not pier outlines or connecting geometry. Any sample must avoid inventing historical relationships.
- Offcut dimensions, condition, and usable yield are unrecorded. Stock quantity alone does not prove suitability.
- No evidence establishes that design, cutting, assembly, and checks fit within four hours.
- Historical demonstrations establish fabrication experience, not accessibility validation.
- `06-operations/2026-rota.txt` explicitly does not establish November availability. Operator slots and staff hours remain conditional.
- `08-admin/weekly-note.txt` requires completion of an existing frame order and a rota update. Listed capacity is not approval to divert staff.
- No participants, partner session, budget for additional purchases, or production arrangement are committed.

### Rights and operating restrictions

WE-98 derivatives require **Harbour Works attribution** and must remain within the documented prototype/presentation permission. Broader distribution or production should have its rights basis confirmed.

The sample must clearly identify itself as **schematic history depicting 1998 features, not current wayfinding**.

Under `04-equipment/fume-guidance.md`, laser work requires extraction and a trained operator. Only approved stock should be used; the listed vinyl and unknown plastics must not enter the laser. Neither the machine record nor the archive supports a certified safety or navigation application.

### Safe next steps and approvals

1. **Prepare a bounded build estimate:** verify access to WE-98, inspect offcut dimensions, select a minimal historically supportable sample, and estimate all work within the four-hour ceiling.
2. **Obtain manager approval** for the internal prototype, staff allocation, operator slot, and existing materials. If these prerequisites fail, stop before fabrication.
3. **Build and internally check one sample:** inspect handling edges, label contrast, stability, and correspondence to the source. Include attribution and the historical-use warning.
4. **Only afterward, seek reading-room agreement** to review proposed materials and discuss participant preferences. Any participant session needs separate scope, staffing, venue, and consent arrangements. Do not promise accessibility benefits.
