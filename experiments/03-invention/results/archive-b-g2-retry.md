## 1. Turn a historic schematic into a tabletop spatial discussion aid

Combine the staff-owned **WE-98 schematic and coordinate export**, retained inclusive-workshop observations, and raised-diagram techniques to explore a tabletop aid explaining how former harbor features related to one another.

The unexpected combination is to use the historic map as a shared spatial object for discussion, with short, high-contrast labels and spoken orientation. This responds to a current difficulty that simply enlarging a handout has not resolved.

### Facts versus hypotheses

**Established:** There is a recent, specific interpretation need; a relevant historic schematic with derivative rights; a coordinate export; retained design observations; and a narrowly defined route to seek internal prototype approval.

**Hypothesis:** A simplified raised layout, accompanied by high-contrast labels and spoken orientation, could make spatial discussion easier for some participants. Neither the old technique sample nor the workshop observations prove that benefit.

The coordinate export identifies positions, but does not establish complete pier outlines or the usability of a finished tactile representation. The actual WE-98 drawing must be inspected before designing a faithful derivative.

### Feasibility and current blockers

- **Concept development is plausible, subject to allocation.** `06-operations/capacity.csv` records three unassigned hours for October 26 and four for November 2, both requiring manager allocation. These are not committed project hours.
- **Laser manufacture is currently blocked.** The November operator slots in `06-operations/laser-access.csv` do not override the failed inspection. February 2027 is an earliest possible availability boundary, not a confirmed return-to-service date.
- **An alternative fabrication route is unverified.** The archive establishes no safe, available substitute for laser cutting. It would be premature to promise hand-cut acrylic or outsourced production.
- **A paper concept needs stock confirmation.** `06-operations/stocktake.md` requires counting unallocated paper; `04-equipment/printer-repair.txt` confirms testing only on standard office paper.
- **Delivery has no commitment or budget.** The reading room expressed interest, not an order. Existing obligations also remain: `08-admin/weekly-note.txt` requires completion of the frame order and other operational tasks.

### Rights and restrictions

The recorded permission supports internal prototyping and presenting proposed educational materials. It does not establish unrestricted publication or distribution rights.

Keep this concept confined to WE-98 unless further permissions are obtained:

### Safe next steps and approvals

1. **Seek manager approval for a bounded feasibility review**, within the four-hour ceiling and subject to current client work. First inspect WE-98, confirm available materials, and estimate the effort.
2. If approved and paper is available, prepare an **internal paper layout or annotated concept** showing the four locations, proposed labels, spoken orientation, and historic-use warning. This would test content and layout only; it would not validate tactile accessibility.
3. Seek authorization before contacting Lian with the concept. Ask whether the proposed approach addresses the reported difficulty and whether members would voluntarily review a future sample. Do not imply a commission, participant commitment, or validated benefit.
4. Defer acrylic fabrication until documented safety clearance and renewed checks of operator availability, stock, cost, and managerial approval. Any external pilot needs separately agreed scope, participation, and delivery arrangements.

**Assessment:** A supported opportunity for inexpensive concept exploration, with a hard block on the documented fabrication method. There is insufficient evidence to promise a usable tactile product, a pilot date, or a funded service.
