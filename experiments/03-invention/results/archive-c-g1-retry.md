# Archive investigation report

**Evaluation date: October 26, 2026**

**No sufficiently supported actionable opportunity was identified.** The archive suggests a potentially useful combination for accessible history learning, but its essential ingredients are restricted, unavailable, or unconfirmed. Presenting it as a viable pilot would overstate the evidence.

I examined the files across all eight workspace directories. No evidence was modified, and no external sources were used.

## Strongest combination considered — not a qualifying opportunity

**Concept:** Combine historical spatial information with high-contrast labels and contrasting tactile textures to create an accessible history-learning object. Paper samples might offer a preliminary way to explore its format.

This combines several distinct resources and observations rather than simply reviving an old programme. However, the archive does not establish current demand, usable content rights, production capacity, or permission to allocate materials.

### Facts versus hypotheses

**Established facts:** Prior participants expressed format preferences; historical coordinates and technique references exist; paper samples are retained. The relevant chart derivatives have restrictive terms, the acrylic demonstration design belongs to its artist, the laser is unavailable, and staff and offcuts are committed.

**Hypotheses:** A new tactile history object could help some learners; paper could support useful early exploration; a future partner might want it. None of those benefits, production methods, or partnerships is demonstrated by the files.

### Feasibility and missing information

**Currently blocked.** A paper version would avoid dependence on the laser, but would not resolve content rights, staff allocation, demand, or design validation.

A future assessment would need:

- A specific intended audience and documented need.
- Identified, cleared historical content or written permission for adaptation.
- An independently developed design or permission for any reused artist design.
- Approved staff time, materials, budget, and space.
- Appropriate user evaluation before making accessibility claims.
- Fresh service clearance and trained-operator availability if laser production is later proposed.

### Safe next step and approvals

Retain this as a **deferred research question**, without commissioning production, promising a session, or contacting partners on the assumption of interest.

If management later allocates time, the first practical step is a short demand-and-rights assessment using the existing records. Management approval is needed for resource allocation; rights-holder permission is needed before adapting restricted content. Any future laser work also requires service clearance and compliance with `04-equipment/fume-guidance.md`. No prototype or outreach was undertaken during this investigation.
