## Strongest rejected candidate: a tactile harbor-history discussion board

**Proposed combination:** turn the owned historic schematic and waypoint data into a raised tabletop diagram, using existing acrylic offcuts and retained workshop techniques, with short contrasting labels and spoken orientation.

This would combine spatial history interpretation with an expressed access need. It is more specific than reproducing an old handout, but **it is not currently a feasible prototype or pilot**.

### Supporting facts

The candidate has support from more than five independent files:

| Exact source path | Relevant evidence |
|---|---|
| `01-correspondence/2026-10-reading-room.txt` | On October 5, the reading room reported that several group members have low vision and struggle to follow relationships between old piers using the large-print handout. It expressed interest in low-cost tabletop approaches, without commissioning materials. |
| `02-archive/catalogue-1998.txt` | WE-98 and its coordinate export were staff-created for a history display. They depict schematic 1998 features, contain no people or private addresses, and are unsuitable for current navigation. |
| `02-archive/1998-waypoints.csv` | Supplies coordinates for four named historic features: Old slip, Warehouse, North jetty, and Market. |
| `03-rights/diagram-terms.txt` | The May 2025 terms authorize physical and digital derivatives of WE-98 for internal prototypes and presentations of proposed non-navigation educational materials, with Harbour Works attribution. |
| `04-equipment/material-log.csv` | Records 18 sheets of approved 3 mm cast acrylic offcuts and 12 water-based paint pens. |
| `05-learning/2019-tile-notes.txt` | Describes stacking laser-cut acrylic shapes into a raised diagram; the sample was never tested as a map or accessibility aid. |
| `05-learning/2018-access-notes.md` | Records informal preferences for large contrasting labels, distinct tactile textures, spoken orientation, and participant review of a sample. |

**Hypothesis:** a raised spatial representation could help participants discuss relationships between historic features. The archive establishes neither accessibility effectiveness nor participant acceptance. Four waypoint coordinates alone also do not establish the full geometry needed for a useful diagram.

### Contradictory evidence and current blockers

- **Fabrication is blocked.** `04-equipment/laser-service.txt`, dated October 14, 2026, says L-2 failed its safety inspection and must remain unavailable until at least February 2027. That date is an earliest boundary, not clearance to resume.
- **Operator availability does not remove the equipment prohibition.** `06-operations/laser-access.csv` lists conditional November 3 and 5 operator slots. `04-equipment/fume-guidance.md` independently requires checking the current service record, extraction, and a trained operator.
- **Capacity is conditional and limited.** `06-operations/capacity.csv` records three unassigned hours for October 26, four for November 2, and two for November 9, with allocation or booking conditions. `06-operations/pilot-limits.txt` permits at most four existing staff hours and existing offcuts for a small internal prototype **if the manager approves**; it provides no committed partners, participants, or external funding.
- **A substitute production route is not evidenced.** The archive does not establish a safe manual acrylic process, another available machine, or an approved fabrication supplier.
- **A paper version cannot be presumed to solve the problem.** `04-equipment/printer-repair.txt` establishes testing on standard office paper only. `06-operations/stocktake.md` requires counting unallocated stock. The reading room already reports difficulty with large print; printing another sheet has no demonstrated benefit.
- **Current serviceability and effort remain unknown.** `02-archive/chart-register.csv` locates WE-98 on the archive drive, but the supplied evidence does not include the drawing itself or establish preparation time.

### Rights, restrictions, and safe next steps

WE-98 offers a documented rights route for an attributed internal prototype and presentation. That permission does not establish unrestricted distribution rights, authorize unrelated third-party material, or make the object suitable for navigation.

A safe next step is a **manager-authorized, time-bounded feasibility review**, not fabrication or a participant pilot:

1. Inspect the WE-98 drawing, check its relationship to the waypoint export, and estimate design effort.
2. Establish whether a safe fabrication route actually exists, including its cost and approvals. Keep L-2 unavailable until formally cleared.
3. If authorized to contact the reading room, clarify the discussion task and whether participants wish to help review a future sample.
4. Seek separate approval for any external session, participant involvement, expenditure, or distribution beyond the documented rights.

These steps could resolve uncertainty; they do **not** make the blocked prototype a qualifying opportunity today.

## Decision

The tactile history board ranks highest for **usefulness, surprise, and evidence strength**, but fails current feasibility. No other candidate clears the evidence threshold.

Retain it as a conditional lead for review after a safe fabrication route is established. Do not book a November build or infer funding from `07-old-programmes/2019-grant-notice.txt`: that call closed in 2019, and `07-old-programmes/old-grant-budget.txt` explicitly does not describe current funding.
