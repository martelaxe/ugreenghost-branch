## 1. A layered tabletop harbour-history sample

### The new combination

Use a **small selection of WE-98’s historic features** to make a raised acrylic sample, paired with large high-contrast labels and a short spoken orientation written from the schematic’s descriptions. Use the sample first to assess fabrication and handling internally. Any later participant review would require separate arrangements.

The unexpected connection is between an old schematic’s coordinate export and a retained layered-acrylic technique: together they could support discussion of spatial relationships that the Reading Room says remain difficult to follow in large print.

### Facts versus hypotheses

**Established:** a current need exists; suitable content has derivative rights; a related construction technique has been demonstrated; approved stock and conditional staff/operator availability are recorded.

**Hypothesis:** raised spatial relationships, high-contrast labels, and spoken orientation could make group discussion easier. The archive contains no evidence that this particular design works for the Reading Room’s participants.

Neither the coordinates nor historical workshop observations justify claiming navigation accuracy, accessibility certification, or a measured benefit.

### Feasibility, restrictions, and blockers

**Feasibility: moderate for a narrowly scoped internal sample; unestablished for an external session.**

The main prerequisites are:

- **Manager allocation:** the four-hour ceiling is an allowance requiring approval, not a project allocation. Operator time must fit within the total effort.
- **Source inspection:** the archive records WE-98 on an archive drive, but the actual drawing is not among the supplied files. Coordinates alone do not supply pier outlines or all spatial relationships.
- **Material and assembly checks:** usable offcut sizes, base construction, joining method, edge finish, and handling suitability remain unknown.
- **Scheduling:** `06-operations/2026-rota.txt` explicitly says September’s rota does not establish November availability. `08-admin/weekly-note.txt` also records existing work commitments.
- **Rights and scope:** attribute Harbour Works and clearly identify the object as a schematic of historical features. The recorded permission supports a prototype and proposal presentation; broader distribution should be checked before proceeding.
- **External participation:** the Reading Room has expressed interest, not agreed to a pilot, venue, date, recruitment, or evaluation.

Distinct textures are a design consideration from past observations, not a capability established by the current materials list.

### Safe next steps and approvals

1. **Prepare a bounded internal specification:** a small sample, explicit historical/non-navigation labelling, attribution, a four-hour maximum, and a stopping point if source access or fabrication cannot be resolved.
2. **Obtain manager approval** for the staff allocation, offcuts, and one recorded trained-operator slot. Confirm whether design, cutting, assembly, and review fit the allowance.
3. **Inspect WE-98 and stock before cutting.** Have the trained operator confirm the fabrication and assembly approach, extraction, and handling checks. Reduce scope or stop if prerequisites fail.
4. **Review internally** for intelligible relationships, label readability, stable construction, and safe handling. Record uncertainties without claiming accessibility effectiveness.
5. **Only after internal review**, seek authorization to approach the Reading Room about voluntary sample feedback. Agree the scope and arrangements with the coordinator before scheduling anything.

No external spending, participant session, or production run is supported at present.
