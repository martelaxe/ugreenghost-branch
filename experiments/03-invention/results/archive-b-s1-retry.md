## Strongest rejected lead: tactile harbor-history tabletop aid

**Proposed combination:** combine the historic schematic’s spatial relationships, retained raised-acrylic techniques, and inclusive workshop observations to help a local history group discuss how former piers related to one another.

This is a substantive cross-file opportunity hypothesis, but **it is blocked rather than ready for a pilot**.

### Hypothesis and potential usefulness

A raised schematic with short, high-contrast labels and spoken orientation **might** make historic spatial relationships easier to discuss. Its unexpected value lies in combining an old exhibition asset with a retained fabrication technique and a current, specific access need.

The files do **not** establish that the design would help this group, that the four coordinates adequately represent the piers, or that participants would accept a review session.

### Contradictions, restrictions, and blockers

- **Fabrication is blocked.** `04-equipment/laser-service.txt`, dated October 14, 2026, says L-2 failed its safety inspection and must remain unavailable until at least February 2027. That date is not a promise of clearance.
- **Operator availability does not override the failure.** `06-operations/laser-access.csv` lists conditional November operator slots, but the machine remains unavailable. `04-equipment/fume-guidance.md` requires checking the current service record, extraction, and a trained operator.
- **Time is conditional.** `06-operations/capacity.csv` lists three unassigned hours for October 26 and four for November 2, requiring allocation; later capacity is subject to client bookings. `06-operations/2026-rota.txt` does not establish November availability.
- **Existing obligations remain.** `08-admin/weekly-note.txt` requires completing an existing frame order and other work.
- **Rights have a defined scope.** The permission in `03-rights/diagram-terms.txt` supports prototypes and proposed-material presentations; it does not establish unrestricted publication or distribution rights.
- **Historical interpretation must remain explicit.** Both `02-archive/catalogue-1998.txt` and the reading-room request prohibit treating the schematic as current wayfinding.
- No file establishes a safe alternative acrylic-cutting method, outsourced fabrication, suitable existing pieces, or an approved alternative budget.

### Safe next steps and approvals

1. Ask the manager to allocate a bounded amount of existing staff time to a **feasibility review**, within the conditional allowance.
2. Check WE-98’s actual content and condition against the coordinate export, and determine which historical relationships need representation.
3. With organizational authorization, invite the coordinator to clarify the access problem and whether participants would consider reviewing a future sample. This would establish interest, not pilot approval.
4. Reassess fabrication only after documented safety clearance, or after a separately evidenced and approved alternative method exists.

Any later pilot would need manager approval, confirmed resources and staffing, partner agreement, voluntary participant involvement, appropriate attribution, and confirmation that the intended use falls within the recorded rights.

## Overall assessment

The tactile schematic ranks highest for **specific usefulness, surprise, and cross-file evidence**, but fails the current feasibility test. The paper/digital substitute has fewer equipment dependencies but substantially weaker evidence of effectiveness and production readiness. The multimedia version introduces unresolved rights.

The defensible action now is a limited feasibility review of the tactile concept. It should not be represented as an approved or feasible pilot.
