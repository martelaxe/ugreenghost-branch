# Red-team assessment

1. **Can generic Codex produce the same ideas?** Yes, in this synthetic experiment: G-A-2 proposed the same kind of cross-project paper-and-text display, and both generic B trials proposed the permitted Tide Lines alternative. The specialized method was safer on one specific B fallback, but one case and two trials do not establish a durable advantage.
2. **Useful or merely imaginative?** The proposals named an actual output, materials, cost and next step from source records. That is actionable on paper; no creator has said it is useful, surprising or feasible in practice.
3. **Help finish something?** A concise draft and permission/venue check can move a fictional project forward. No actual artifact, booking or production occurred, and a competent generic assistant already produced comparable directions.
4. **Does restriction checking add value?** The B variants show why a restriction can change a recommendation. Structured prompting avoided one flagged risky workaround in this sample, yet its C report had broken links and a false inventory count. This does not establish a safe or superior system.
5. **Does a NAS matter?** It can hold a creator's accumulated, authorized project files and expose documented native expert capabilities. A generic assistant can inspect a mounted folder too; no measured setup, privacy or usability advantage was established. Local storage does not prove local inference.
6. **Different from Adobe-style DAM?** Adobe advertises retrieval, reuse and rights management, while Contesimal advertises transforming historical libraries into new opportunities. A tailored, lightweight chat proposal across a small personal archive is a possible distinction, not proof the competitors cannot do it. No pricing, product setup or functionality comparison was conducted hands-on.
7. **Strongest reason judges reject it:** A chatbot prompt over folders producing creative suggestions plus familiar rights disclaimers; no distinct technical mechanism, independent user preference or reliable agent runtime is shown.
8. **Strongest reason judges remember it:** A specific forgotten creative project becomes a small executable display, while a genuine restriction changes the recommendation to a different plan. It must be surprising to actual creators; our fixture authors seeded the connection.
9. **Deliverable with official Skill?** A read-only text proposal is plausible from documented file, document, knowledge and optionally photo agents. Runtime provenance, source coverage, file formats, identity/rights matching and user-decision resumption are unverified. Generated visuals, video, autonomous publication and custom executable backends cannot be assumed.
10. **Could a simpler concept be better?** Yes. A Knowledge Succession Rehearsal has a clearer concrete user and a text-only path, although generic AI can do it and real-user/NAS proof is also missing. Fork & Finish gives a crisp choice between conditional outcomes but generic Codex already achieved it in experiment 08. Creative Salvage should earn its additional complexity in a creator test, not by default.
