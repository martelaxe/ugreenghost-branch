# Experiment 09 — results

**Conclusion: no reliable creative advantage over generic Codex is established.** The specialized method did somewhat better in this particular blind AI review: its two restriction-case reports avoided the particular unsupported fallback that one generic run proposed. But both methods independently found useful alternatives and made notable mistakes; this is a six-run-per-method, correlated, synthetic text fixture, evaluated by AI rather than real creators. The observed difference merits examination, not a claim of superiority.

## Executed experiment

- Three fictional archive variants, **31 text files each**, with two abandoned projects, constraints, scattered records, decoys, one misleading old pattern and an unavailable audio asset. All files are fictional; no real photos, recordings, videos or stencil assets were supplied. [Frozen key](keys/answer-key.md) and [design](benchmark-design.md).
- **12/12 fresh investigator processes completed**, 2 per method per variant, each configured as `gpt-6-luna` / low, one process per response; generic G and structured S received the same files and task with S's additional workflow instructions. Backend routing was not independently attested. Input hashes were unchanged. Raw responses, exact prompts, JSONL events, stderr and hashes: [execution](execution/).
- After investigator completion, report labels were randomized and workspace prefixes normalized. Three fresh, case-specific `gpt-6.1-sol` high evaluator processes each assessed four anonymous reports against the case archive and frozen key. Evaluators did not receive the method mapping. All 3 completed and produced parseable scoring JSON. These are separate AI contexts sharing a provider/model family, **not independent people**; style could reveal treatment. [Blinded reports](review/blinded/), [scorer outputs and mapping](review/).
- The human creator evaluation has **not been conducted**. [Protocol](human-evaluation-protocol.md) and three participant packets exist; none was sent.

### Concrete proposal and alternative

The most coherent proposed output is **a participatory changing-shoreline table** combining Folded Water's moving-paper activity with Tide Lines captions and observations, while confirming current supplies, a library date and the makers' credits. [Generic A-G-2](execution/A-G-2.md) proposed this explicitly; [specialized A-S-1](execution/A-S-1.md) also proposed it. Source checks: `01-projects/folded-water/workshop.txt`, `01-projects/tide-lines/text-fragments.txt`, and both `03-rights` records. This is a synthetic textual concept, not a fabricated finished exhibit; it is not established as surprising to a creator.

When the FW-7 restriction changes in B, [specialized B-S-1](execution/B-S-1.md) proposes a text-only Tide Lines display based on `03-rights/tide-notes.txt`, with $8 printing and a venue still to confirm, while stating that a workshop does not bypass the pattern restriction. [Generic B-G-1](execution/B-G-1.md) also proposes the same separate text-only alternative. Therefore **discovery of a viable alternative was not unique to the specialized prompt**. Their practical value, creativity, cost and actual rights implications have not been independently tested outside the fictional record.

The B-G-1 report also says: “If permission is unavailable, develop a display from the general movable-band idea without reproducing FW-7.” The blind evaluator flagged this as a serious potential evasion of the explicit adaptation restriction; whether a legally independent idea exists cannot be determined from the fixture. This is a reason to request review, not a legal conclusion. [B-G-2](execution/B-G-2.md) makes the public demonstration conditional on permission, although its placement of fabrication before the condition is ambiguous. Neither specialized B run made the same fallback claim. A tiny controlled sample cannot establish comparative safety rates.

### Citation, quote and coverage audit

A deterministic post-review [Markdown link check](review/automated-link-audit.json) checked **122 generic and 140 specialized link targets** against their assigned fixtures. Within the investigator namespace, G: 0 broken targets; S: **4 malformed compound link targets in C-S-2**. The blind evaluator noticed malformed formatting but considered individual embedded paths identifiable and did not flag unresolvable citations. Our mechanical audit finds the Markdown targets themselves do not resolve, so we record this as an additional serious citation-integrity failure **without changing the blind score**. This check maps `/tmp/work/` back to the particular investigator workspace, so it measures the links as they existed inside that run. All 12 raw reports use `/tmp/work/` absolute paths, which are no longer mounted at that path on the host: **none of those raw links are directly portable after the process exits**. This is a common source-delivery failure across both methods, even where the underlying relative file exists; the blinded and human packets deliberately normalize the workspace prefix to `archive/`. The four malformed targets remain malformed after normalization. Bare filenames and claim–citation pairing were not fully machine-audited. A run having no bad internal targets does not prove every claim was cited or entailed.

The [quote-candidate audit](review/automated-quote-candidates.json) checks literal substrings, not truth. Two unmatched candidates were inspected: A-S-2's “Changing Shoreline” is a new proposal title, not an attributed quotation; B-S-1's “A crossing changes with the tide,” changes the source's final period to a comma inside the quotes. This minor punctuation change is not a substantive invented quotation, but it is not an exact string match. The anonymous AI reviewers separately assessed semantic support against source files; that judgment is fallible and does not establish legal compliance.

C-S-2 said it reviewed **27 files**; captured command output actually inventories **31** and subsequently cats the 31. The qualifier that the inventory may be incomplete does not correct the wrong count. C-G-1's opening statement that both concepts fit one weekend/$30 is not established when pricing, venue, materials and approval are unknown, even though it later says they are not ready for public use. These are concrete evidence/coverage weaknesses.

C-S-2 calls for a private mock-up and an independent activity that draws on written observations, while the fixture leaves adaptation and public use of some contributions restricted. It later identifies the public-use restrictions, but whether its proposed activity is independent and any particular private adaptation is permitted cannot be resolved from the fixture. Treat this as **additional ambiguity needing human clarification**, not a proven legal violation or verified safe workaround. C-S-1 similarly calls for a newly designed activity; distinctness is a proposed constraint, not verified permission.

## Isolation, effort and limitations

Investigators ran in separate bubblewrap mount namespaces: project repository (including keys, other variants and reports) was hidden, `/tmp` was replaced, and only the assigned 31-file workspace and its output directory were bind-mounted. The read-only Codex sandbox was also selected. This addresses Experiment 08's late permission restriction. A [preflight](preflight/) successfully read a one-file workspace while the project path was hidden. A separate post-run check did not observe access to the hidden key through the probed parent `/proc` path; it is not a security proof. Other host paths and Codex auth/runtime directories remained visible or bound for the CLI; the same OS user and backend were shared. This is materially stronger workspace/key isolation, not a proof of complete information security or no residual leakage.

The shell-event audit observed no `/home/` or `/mnt/` paths in completed investigator shell commands, nor recorded web or MCP calls; that simple check cannot prove no access via an unlogged mechanism. Web search was disabled in configuration; OS-level networking was not independently disabled because the CLI needs the model service. 31-file input hashes match before and after all runs. Different trial runs listed/catted files in different ways. All 12 output files and all three blinded scorer outputs were obtained.

One model call per investigator; three additional blinded scorer calls. Logged investigator usage ranged approximately 50,464–51,227 input tokens and 969–1,635 output tokens per run, with different cached token counts. These counters are not billed costs, and 12 concurrent elapsed times (22.19–31.34 s) are not a comparative speed study. No real-world originality, quality, business value, public-display legality, UGREEN agent execution, image understanding, interactive resumption, or real-user preference was tested.

**Design limits:** The archives are nearly identical variants authored by the experimenter; the strongest theme is seeded in project notes, source terms are simple, and the positive idea can be guessed by proximity. Human and independent authors may find it ordinary. The blinded evaluators shared a simple key and rubric, one evaluator per case; scoring looks ceiling-saturated and no human inter-rater reliability exists. The prompt-length difference is intentional treatment. A more demanding real creator archive and a participant-blinded comparison are essential before any claim of differentiation.
