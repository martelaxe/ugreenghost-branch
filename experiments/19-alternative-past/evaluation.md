# Experiment 19 — execution and temporal tests

## Design and boundary

Fictional scenario: a community board temporarily moved children's reading sessions from the Harbor Reading Room to a school hall after receiving an official safety order identifying an obstructed exit. Cutoff: **2024-09-12 23:59 UTC**. The order, delivery receipt, minutes, a location question, city desk reply, competing access needs and other records were in a hand-built fictional snapshot. Eleven records plus the snapshot inventory were placed in a separate THEN workspace. Later or uncertain items were absent, including a fieldbook and city correction that identify the inspected site as Harbor Annex, 72 Alder Street. The full archive contained 20 fictional records plus the same snapshot. [Availability table](availability-table.md) and [hidden author-created chronology](fixtures/private/ground-truth.md).

Four fresh `codex exec` processes requested `gpt-6.1-sol` with low reasoning and read-only workspaces. THEN got only the cutoff snapshot. NOW-P and NOW-G got byte-identical full archives and the same competent task request; P received additional temporal classification and reveal instructions. NOW-P-IRRELEVANT got the same P prompt and archive plus one October poster. Exact [prompts, raw responses, event logs and execution records](runs/) are retained. All four exited 0 with two read-only shell commands each, and input hashes were unchanged. Known project and host temporary paths were hidden by an outer namespace, and known history/attachment directories masked. Other host paths, configured runtime and network isolation were not fully verified, so this is not perfect isolation. The fixture is fictional and newly created; it reduces but cannot formally eliminate model-memory leakage. Source files and hashes do not constitute an independently authenticated historical record.

The THEN output described the order as an apparent safety concern, the documented location question and desk's instruction to follow the written order while awaiting confirmation; it gave relocation, suspension and clarification options and reported the actual temporary move. It did not predict or know the later correction. This is one AI's interpretation, not a reconstructed human mental state. The NOW-P response used the September 20 fieldbook, September 21 photo ledger and September 23 correction to identify the inspection as concerning **72 Alder Street, not 27**. It still qualified the original move as a precaution amid uncertainty and did not infer that 27 was safe on September 12.

## Four requested tests

| Test | Actual observation | Result and limit |
|---|---|---|
| A: hindsight leakage | THEN had only the 11 attested records; its response did not state 72 Alder, transposition, the fieldbook, or later corrections. Manual review found no material future fact. | Pass for these known markers and this response; cannot rule out every unobserved external knowledge pathway. |
| B: backdated document | NOW-P and NOW-G explicitly said `17_backdated_memo.md` describes August but was created and added October 18. | Pass: neither treated it as cutoff evidence. The October memo does not establish September 12 safety. |
| C: uncertain availability | Both identified `18_uncertain_carbon.md` as unknown: a handwritten September 10 date and October discovery do not prove before-cutoff access or post-cutoff creation. | Pass on this explicit case. THEN did not receive the note, so did not mistakenly use its contents. |
| D: irrelevant later evidence | A fresh NOW-P run received the identical corpus plus `99_art_poster.md`; it labeled the poster irrelevant and kept the same central wrong-building finding and September safety caveat. | Pass for one constructed control. Wording varied, and there is no proof of general robustness. |

[Mechanical checks](tests.json) verify snapshot hashes, missing later fixture files, identical G/P full archives, one-file mutation, and selected keywords. The markers cannot establish semantic correctness, judge intent, or prove no outside access. Manual review supports the statements above. The after-cutoff answer key and case controls were prepared before investigator outputs and were not present in investigator workspaces; all fixture assertions are constructed facts, not real-world evidence.

## Generic versus procedure

G and P both distinguished the official order at 27 from the later fieldbook/correction at 72, noted the contemporary location question, dated the backdated memo by archive entry, marked the carbon's availability unknown, and declined to infer September safety from an October inspection. Both characterized the move as a temporary precaution rather than proof of malice. P formatted a detailed per-record THEN/REVEAL/NOW classification; G produced a substantially equivalent interpretation and ordered evidence table. More elaborate output or the research-only reveal interface is not evidence P reasoned better. One sample per method cannot prove equivalence generally or detect small differences.

The local [HTML prototype](visual-review/index.html) pairs the actual THEN and NOW-P results with direct relative links to copied fixture source records, and [overview](visual-review/overview.png) shows the change. Its show/hide interaction is a storytelling interface, not an information-security boundary; the full later files ship with the local review. Original raw model responses use temporary `/tmp/work/` Markdown links that do not resolve after the sandbox exits. The HTML constructs new links to retained source copies; it does not silently alter the raw model responses. Source links were checked mechanically, and control behavior was tested with a mocked DOM—not an actual browser rendering or user study.
