The board’s September 12 decision was a temporary precaution based on an official order whose location was already disputed. Later records establish that the order named the wrong building. They change the factual explanation for the move, but do not establish that the board knew of the error when it acted—or that the reading room’s exit was clear on September 12.

**What was available at the cutoff**

[SNAPSHOT.md](/tmp/work/SNAPSHOT.md) attests that records 01–10 and 20 were in the board archive by **2024-09-12 23:59 UTC**. It explicitly distinguishes archive presence from individual readership and describes its hashes as fixture identifiers, not independent authentication.

The central evidence was:

- At **09:12 UTC**, the archive received Order CR-41, naming **Harbor Reading Room, 27 Alder Street**, reporting an obstructed west exit, and directing that public activities stop until the concern was resolved. [01_board_notice.md](/tmp/work/01_board_notice.md) and [02_intake_receipt.md](/tmp/work/02_intake_receipt.md) corroborate receipt.
- By **13:02**, a board message explicitly questioned whether the order concerned the reading room or **Harbor Annex**. At **14:31**, the city desk acknowledged the question, said the inspector was unavailable, and instructed: “Follow the written order until the Safety Office confirms the location.” Thus uncertainty was documented before the decision, but no correction had arrived. [06_board_question.md](/tmp/work/06_board_question.md), [07_desk_reply.md](/tmp/work/07_desk_reply.md).
- At the **16:00 meeting**, the chair read the location and exit concern from CR-41. The board temporarily moved children’s reading sessions to the school hall “pending clarification.” The minutes, archived at **16:10**, expressly exclude a decision to sell or permanently close the building. [03_board_minutes.md](/tmp/work/03_board_minutes.md). The **18:00** public notice likewise described a temporary move and hoped-for return. [09_public_notice.md](/tmp/work/09_public_notice.md).

On that evidence, the decision is reasonably interpreted as compliance with a written safety restriction while seeking clarification. That is an interpretation of the documented sequence, not proof of every member’s reasoning.

The alternative had practical costs and limits. A tutor warned of longer walks and requested a morning session at Harbor, while saying not to use a blocked exit. The school offered Saturday space for two weeks but no buses; its accessible entrance and route assistance did not resolve transport planning. The budget allowed one weekend hall rental, leaving the funding of a second weekend unexplained. These constraints were available before the meeting. [04_tutor_message.md](/tmp/work/04_tutor_message.md), [05_school_offer.md](/tmp/work/05_school_offer.md), [08_accessibility_note.md](/tmp/work/08_accessibility_note.md), [20_finance.md](/tmp/work/20_finance.md). Harbor also normally hosted crafts and quiet study, but the minutes establish only the reading-session relocation, not what happened to those other activities. [10_room_calendar.md](/tmp/work/10_room_calendar.md).

Two records require particular caution. [17_backdated_memo.md](/tmp/work/17_backdated_memo.md) bears an August 28 heading but was created and entered **October 18**, retrospectively from interviews. Its volunteer recollection concerns late August and expressly does not establish September 12 conditions. [18_uncertain_carbon.md](/tmp/work/18_uncertain_carbon.md) bears a handwritten September 10 date but has unknown authorship, distribution, and archive availability; discovery in an undated box on October 10 proves neither earlier receipt nor later creation. It cannot establish advance knowledge of the error. The October bus adjustment is also later evidence and does not resolve either September transport arrangements or the inspection’s location. [19_october_transport.md](/tmp/work/19_october_transport.md).

The later evidence therefore replaces an apparent inspection finding against the reading room with a documented administrative error concerning the Annex. It does **not** turn the earlier relocation into evidence of knowing misrepresentation, prove September safety at number 27, erase the move’s access burdens, or change the documented temporary character of the decision. The archive supports a mistaken official premise and a precautionary response under acknowledged uncertainty; claims about concealed motives or individual beliefs would go beyond these records.
