Why did the reading room move?

A decision at the edge of a safety order. First see the record at the time; then open the later evidence.

This is a fictional scenario, not an actual historical event. “Available” means recorded in the fictional board archive—not proof of what every person read, knew or believed. The interactive reveal is presentation, not an access-control boundary.

12 September 2024 · 23:59 UTC

At the decision

The order the board archive had received

“Location shown on order: Harbor Reading Room, 27 Alder Street.”

It described an obstructed exit and told the board to stop public activities until the concern was resolved. Read the order · See the receipt

But the location was already uncertain.

A board message asked whether the order concerned the Reading Room or Harbor Annex. The city desk had not confirmed the location and told the board to follow the order meanwhile.

Board question · Desk reply

Recorded decision

The chair read the order's location and exit concern; the board moved the children’s reading sessions to a school hall temporarily. Longer walks and transport costs remained concerns. Meeting minutes · Tutor’s concern

23 September 2024 · 14:00 UTC

The reveal

A later city correction arrived in the archive.

“Correction to order CR-41: the Sept 11 inspection applied to 72 Alder Street, Harbor Annex.”

The correction says the address on the original order had been transposed. The fieldbook and photo ledger had arrived on September 20–21 and identified the Annex. Read the correction · Read the fieldbook · Read the photo ledger

This newly received evidence corrects which building CR-41 inspected. It does not establish that the Reading Room exit was clear on September 12.

The retrospective view

A different explanation, not a different past

The notice named the wrong building.

“Later records establish that CR-41 misidentified the Reading Room.” NOW agent’s actual response

Records available at the decision still show an official order, a location question and advice to follow it while awaiting confirmation. Later correction does not prove that the board knew the address was wrong.

Two time traps

An August 28 account was prepared and entered on October 18; its old subject date does not make it available in September. Read the later account

A note dated September 10 has unknown authorship and receipt history; discovery in October does not establish whether it was available before the decision. Read the uncertain note

An October 4 inspection found a clear exit on that day; it does not prove the exit was clear in September. Read the inspection
