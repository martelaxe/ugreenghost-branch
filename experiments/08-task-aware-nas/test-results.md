# Benchmark results: four cases, eight fresh processes

Executed 2026-10-26. **The specialized workflow did not demonstrate an advantage.** Both approaches identified the central fact pattern in all four small synthetic scenarios; the specialized condition introduced broken absolute citations in the conflict scenario and asked an unnecessary approval question in the recoverable scenario. Scores are descriptive and author-judged, not statistically meaningful.

**Serious failures:** C-S used five non-existent absolute citation targets, recorded as an invented/invalid source-link defect. The issue is a broken target for genuinely read evidence, not proof that the underlying statements or filenames were fabricated. No report is observed to issue an unsafe critical instruction, make a false assertion of complete archive coverage, or access an unauthorized folder. We did not conduct an exhaustive sentence-level factual audit beyond the benchmark items.

## Execution and limitations

All eight fresh `codex exec` processes exited 0 and produced final reports; each was requested as `gpt-6-luna` with low reasoning, `--ignore-user-config`, `--ephemeral`, `--sandbox read-only` and web search disabled. CLI events did not independently confirm backend identity. One model process per result; no UGREEN expert ran. Fixture checksums before and after match for all runs. Exact prompts, reports, events, stderr, status and hashes are in [execution](execution/).

The investigator workspaces were copied under `/tmp`, contained only each case's files and no symlinks, and were explicitly restricted by prompt. The answer key and preregistration were permission-restricted **after processes were launched**, leaving a brief initial race; read-only is not a full filesystem-read boundary, and other cases were present in the shared filesystem. Captured command events show workspace-relative reads only, with no recorded web or external call; that is evidence of behavior, not proof of perfect isolation or no unobserved access. Thus this is an independent fresh-context comparison with **partial, not guaranteed, blinding**. Source material includes the scope restriction as a fictional file; no real inaccessible NAS folder or runtime search failure was exercised. The generic and specialized instructions differ in length. Output reporting is one-shot: a requested user decision is not tested through an actual continuation. The evaluator is also the study author and knew the condition names. These limitations preclude claiming a blind, independent human evaluation or a general efficacy result.

The fixtures and rubric are synthetic, invoice-heavy, small, and explicitly make key facts easy to find. They do not test hundreds of files, unusual layouts, diverse industries, image handling, human adoption, prompt-injection robustness, or independent reproduction. A different model, larger dataset or repeated trials could produce different outcomes. The next meaningful test should exercise a new user-visible output and compare the same access/budget against a generic baseline, plus actual UGREEN source handoffs when possible.
